# Chinese (Simplified): answers to the language questionnaire

**Amended 2026-11-19** (Claude Fable 5.2), after Simplified and
Traditional Chinese, written independently, were compared pattern by
pattern (887 patterns). Where the two differed for a reason that was not
regional, one had usually found the better form. Every lead below came
from that comparison; each choice was then made again from this
language's own usage notes, and where the form is the sister's in other
characters that is said, with the reason. No wrong schedule was found.
Passages changed are marked "(2026-11-19)". Each old and new sentence was
compared by hand on the four costs of `CLAUDE.md` (spoken length, objects
held, repetition, operations); the comparisons are summarised where the
choice is recorded. None of the new forms has been read by a fluent
human; the reading check that followed is recorded in `status.json`.

* **A cadence of hours that tiles the day from 0:00 is its step** (2.5,
  3.7, 4.1): 每2小时一次, 每3小时的15分, 每6小时的5分、35分. This undoes
  the second and third amendments of 2026-11-14, which rested on two
  model readers who took the bare step as counted from when the task
  starts. That objection is the one the project has settled against: a
  cadence that begins on the first hour of the day is not qualified, in
  any language (README, "Description Accuracy"; `CLAUDE.md`, "How quality
  is judged"), and a reader's wish to be told where it starts decides
  nothing. Four forms had grown for this one kind (偶数整点, a stated
  start, a window, a list).
* **A stretch of hours before named minutes is said once**, by its first
  and last firing (4.1): 9:05至17:45，每小时的5分、20分、45分. The bracket
  （首次9:05，末次17:45） said the stretch a second time, after hours by
  name (9点至17点) that were not its ends.
* **No series is left to inference** (3.7): the …… is gone. Every second
  date between two ends is 8日至30日逢双日; other cadences of dates are
  listed.
* Smaller: a date that not every month has is 每逢31日; 每逢闰年2月29日;
  a day counted back from the month's end is its date where that is one
  date; the weekday nearest a date says which way it moves; weekdays
  across the weekend are 每周五至次周周一 and dates across the month's
  end 每月14日至次月10日; a date or a weekday that leaves one or two days
  out is 除周三外每天（周三为1日时照常）; every second month is 奇数月, 偶
  数月;
  three hours or more that hold minutes are named once; a rhythm with a
  beat missing is 每10分钟一次（每小时的30分除外）.

**Amended 2026-11-14 on the maintainer's rulings** (Claude Opus 5.5).
Until this date the book said an hour cadence only with its times: every
second hour was 每天0:00至22:00，每2小时一次, every third hour a list of
eight times, every second hour under minutes 偶数小时（0点、2点、4点……22
点）内 with a gloss of the hours, every sixth hour under every minute four
windows, and a cadence of years carried a bracket of its first years. That
was wrong. README "Description Accuracy" says the library does not qualify
a cadence that begins on the first second, minute or hour of its cycle,
and a cadence of hours that tiles the day from 0:00 is such a cadence: the
window, the list and the gloss only repeat what 每2小时 already says, and a
reader has to read them through to find that they add nothing. The
earlier reader evidence for windows, glosses and stated starts (3.7, 4.1,
5.5, 5.8) is overruled: isolated model readers have proven to favour
verbose, over-qualified wording that people do not. Now an hour cadence
that tiles the day is said by its step alone (3.7, 4.1), a minute or a
second with it is said as against every hour (每2小时的30分), its hours
as stretches under a finer rhythm are 偶数小时内 or 每6小时的第1个小时内,
no bracket shows a series of hours or years (5.5, 5.8), and seconds that
step evenly at one clock time are a window with the step (4.2). The
passages changed are marked "(2026-11-14)".

**Amended again the same day** (Claude Opus 5.5), because three of those
forms have a second reading. Two isolated readers (Opus 5.5 and Sonnet
5.5), briefed only in Chinese, each found that 每2小时一次 and 每2小时的5
分、35分 can be counted from whenever the task was set up, which may be
the odd hours; one read 自1点起每2小时的15分 as from 1 p.m.; both read 每
天2点至22点的偶数小时的0分至29分 as able to end at 22:00, leaving out
22:01 to 22:29. In Chinese 每N小时一次 times a dose or a check from when
it starts (每2小时一次 on a medicine label); it is not a clock reading, and
a reader who has not read the README is not told where it starts. Asked
again, the same two readers read 每6小时一次 and 每3小时的15分 two ways as
well. So no cadence of hours is said by its step alone. Every second hour
is the even or the odd hours, with 每天: 每天偶数整点, 每天奇数小时的15分,
每天偶数小时内，每分钟一次. Every sixth or eighth hour is four times or
fewer and is listed (每天0:00、6:00、12:00、18:00); every third or fourth
hour at one minute is said from its first time with 每天 (每天0:00起每3
小时一次; third amendment, 3.7), and with several minutes its hours are
named. The even or odd hours between two ends close on their first and last firing in brackets, as one range of
hours does: 每天2点至22点，偶数小时的0分至29分，每分钟一次（首次2:00，末
次22:29）. Both readers read every new form one way. The ruling against
brackets that show a series stands: none of these does. Passages changed
are marked "(2026-11-14, second amendment)".

Answered by the orchestrating model (Claude Fable 5.2), 2026-11-12, for

Nothing here was carried over from another language. Where the two judges

Shorthand: **M** is the set of minutes, **H** the set of hours, **S** the
set of seconds.

## What runs through everything

* **A field that takes every value is silent**, with two fillers for a
  level the sentence would otherwise skip: 每天 (2.5) and 每年 (2.6).
* **The aim is a sentence that is easy to understand.** Exact times need
  no working out. A stretch of consecutive values needs none either. A
  cadence with a step needs arithmetic, unless it starts where its cycle
  starts and the reader takes it so: 每15分钟 is taken from the hour and
  每2小时 from midnight, which is what README "Description Accuracy"
  leaves unsaid. (2026-11-19. On 2026-11-14 two model readers read 每2小
  时一次 as counted from when the task starts, and the first judge had
  read 每12小时 as "does not say from when"; for five days hours were
  never said by a bare step. A reader who asks from when is asking for
  what the convention leaves out, and that is not a second reading.) An
  exception needs subtraction. A list needs nothing, but every item has
  to be held.
* **Every stretch is closed and names its first and last moment, and both
  fire** (8.5). This one rule decides two contested forms: an hour is
  9:00至9:59, not 9点这一小时内; a minute is 9:00:00至9:00:59, not 9:00这一
  分钟内.
* **An instant is a clock time with a colon (9:00); a whole hour is named
  with 点 (9点).** 点 is used in exactly one place, a whole hour and never
  an instant: in a list of separate hours before 的 and what happens in
  them (8点、12点的15分; 0点、6点、12点、18点的每一分钟). Everywhere else an
  hour is its clock times. (2026-11-19: it also stood in a range of hours
  before 每小时的, 9点至17点，每小时的…, and at the ends of the even or
  odd hours, 2点至22点，偶数小时; both are now the stretch from the first
  firing to the last, 4.1. 2026-11-14: a bracket of hours after 偶数小时
  and the start of an hour cadence, 自1点起每2小时一次, were tried and
  withdrawn, the second when a reader took 1点 for 13:00.)
* **What can be counted is listed** ("凡是数得过来的，细心的人会直接列出
  来"). The limits are stated here once, by kind of value, and hold
  everywhere.

| Values | Listed | A run is a range from | More, on a step | An exception replaces |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| clock times in a day; minutes named in the hour; seconds named in the minute | up to 8, except seconds that step at one clock time (4.2), and except hours on a step that tiles the day from 0:00 (3.7) | 5 consecutive (4 or fewer are listed) | hours that tile the day from 0:00: the step, 每2小时一次 (3.7); the odd hours: the idiom, 每天奇数整点 (5.5); any other: both ends and the step, 5:00至23:00，每2小时一次 | a list of more than 8; or a rhythm that tiles with one or two beats missing and more than 4 left (3.9) |
| dates of the month | up to 8; a cadence that is not every second date, however many | 3 consecutive (two are listed) | every second date: the idiom (5.1), or between two ends 8日至30日逢双日 | a list of more than 8 |
| months | always named, except every second month (5.5) | 3 consecutive (two are listed) | every second month: 奇数月, 偶数月; no other cadence | more than 8 months that are not one run |
| weekdays | always named | 3 consecutive (two are listed) | no cadence: named | exactly six days that are not one run |
| years | up to 8 | 3 consecutive (two are listed) | 自2028年起每4年中的第1年 (5.8) | more than 8 years with one or two missing (5.8) |

  A run of clock times waits for five because each is a separate firing
  and four are quicker read than worked out (9:00、10:00、11:00); a run of
  days, months or years is a span and is one from three (6月至8月).
    A set that has no shorter description under these rules is listed,
  however long, with two bounds. Clock times, minutes, seconds and years
  that are runs and cadences together are said as those (3.8, 5.8), so
  that no list of them passes what the field holds in a day, an hour or a
  minute. And what is bounded is a list made by multiplying two lists:
  separate hours are a window of minutes each only while there are two,
  and hours each with several minutes are clock times only while there
  are two (4.1; 2026-11-19, it was four); clock times or minutes each with
  their seconds are written out only while that names six things or
  fewer, a window counting two (4.2).
* **A cadence of minutes or seconds that tiles from 0 is 每N分钟一次 / 每N
  秒一次 with no ends** when nothing confines it: 每15分钟一次. Neither
  judge asked where it starts; everyone takes it from the hour. A cadence
  of hours that tiles the day from 0:00 is 每N小时一次 in the same way
  (3.7; 2026-11-19).
* **The choice never depends on which values are named**, only on how
  many there are and how they are arranged, except for the idioms of
  section 5 and what the calendar decides (4.4): a date its months do not
  have, and a 29 February.
* **No ties are declared** (3.11). Equal schedules are said alike however
  the pattern was written.

## 1. Anchors

1.1 The practice of mainland Chinese software interfaces and timetables:
the 24-hour clock with a colon, Arabic digits, 年 月 日 dates, 周一 to 周日
for the days of the week. Punctuation follows GB/T 15834—2011 and numerals
GB/T 15835—2011: full-width 、，；：（）。. (The six-dot ellipsis …… was
used for an abbreviated series until 2026-11-19; no series is abbreviated
now, 3.7.)

1.2 The style table. Each field changes words or the form of one phrase,
never the order of the sentence.

| Field | Kind | Default | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hour12` | boolean | `false` | `false`: 24-hour times (17:30) and hour names (17点). `true`: 12-hour times and hour names, each with the part of the day before it: 下午5:30, 下午5点 |
| `short` | boolean | `false` | Nothing: the sentence is the same with it on or off. Chinese names of weekdays and months have no shorter written form (周一, 6月). The field exists only so that a caller can set it for every language alike |

With `hour12: true` the hour is 0, 1 … 12, 1 … 11 and every clock time and
every hour name, wherever it stands (in a list, a window, an exception
bracket, or before 点), is preceded by its part of the day: 凌晨 for hours
0 to 4, 早上 for 5 to 7, 上午 for 8 to 11, 中午 for 12, 下午 for 13 to 17,
晚上 for 18 to 23. 早上6:00, 上午9:00, 中午12:00, 下午5:45, 晚上10:00, 上午8
点. (上午 ran from 6 until the second repair. The notes put 早上 before
it, as speech does: 上午6:00 is not wrong, but nobody says it.) It is never shared:
上午9:00至下午5:45. 次日 comes before the part of the day: 晚上10:00至次日凌
晨2:59.

A time in the hour after midnight keeps hour 0: 凌晨0:00, 凌晨0:30, 凌晨0
点. (凌晨12:00 is colloquial and can be taken for noon.)

1.3 None yet. Traditional Chinese (台湾, 香港) differs in script and in some
words (每週, 分鐘, 小時) and is left for a later region.

## 2. Frame

2.1 One order, coarsest part first: **year, then month, then day, then
time**. It is the order of a Chinese date and time (2030年6月1日9:00), and
no other order is Chinese (9:00每天 is not said). The parts are written
one after another with no space.

Between the day and the time stands a **join**, declared here and measured
by `test/lang/zh/continuity.js`. It is one of: nothing, a comma, 每天, 的,
，均在, ，均为.
It depends only on what stands before the time and on whether the time
runs round the clock: the hours are every hour, or a rhythm of hours
(3.7: a cadence that tiles the day from 0:00 whose minutes are moments),
with or without exceptions. Take the first row that applies:

| The time part | What stands before it | Join |
| --- | --- | --- |
| any | nothing | none |
| none: the schedule never fires (4.4) | nothing | none |
| runs round the clock, so it says a rhythm and names no clock time first | a year part that ends in 当年 (5.8) | none: …，当年每5分钟一次 |
| the same | anything else | a comma: 每周一至周五，每15分钟一次; 每月1日，每小时整点; 每年6月，每5分钟一次; 每月1日，每2小时一次; 每周一，每2小时的30分 |
| the same | a date and a weekday together (4.3) | ，均为: 每月1日以及每周五，均为每分钟一次; 每月1日以及每周五，均为每2小时一次 |
| begins with a clock time, an hour name or the even or odd hours | no day part (a month or a year only) | 每天 (2.5): 每天9:00; 每年6月每天9:00; 每天奇数整点; 每天偶数小时内，每分钟一次 |
| the same | a date and a weekday together (4.3) | ，均在: 每月1日以及每周五，均在0:00; 每月1日以及每周五，均在奇数整点 |
| the same | a date and a weekday that must both match (4.3) | 的: 每逢单日为周一时的0:00 |
| the same | a day part that is exactly one range of dates | 每天: 每月1日至7日每天9:00 |
| the same | dates in several pieces, at least one a range | 的: 每月1日至7日、15日的9:00 |
| the same | anything else: one date, a list of dates, 逢单日 or 8日至30日逢双日, a weekday phrase, a Quartz form, 除…外每天, and any of these closed by a bracket (4.3, 4.4) | none: 每月1日、15日9:00; 每周一至周五9:00; 除周三外每天9:00; 每逢31日0:00; 每月1日奇数整点; 每周一至周五偶数小时内，每15分钟一次; 除周三外每天（周三为1日时照常）0:00 |

Reasons. The first judge found strings of 每… with no pause "挤成一串" and
asked for the comma before a frequency; the second graded those forms
natural. A range of dates followed directly by a time (1日至7日9:00) is
read as a span that ends at 9:00 on the 7th; 每天 makes that reading
impossible (both judges: natural). After pieces, 每天 would stand against
a single date (15日每天, a clash the schedule reader pointed out), so 的
carries the time to all of them.

So 的 stands between the days and the time where the day part is dates
in pieces, and after 为…时: a time set directly against the last item
would be read with that item alone, and 的 after a list of one kind is
read with the whole list. Where the day part is two kinds of day joined
by 以及 (4.3), 的 is not enough, and ，均在 stands instead: 每月1日以及每
周五，均在0:00. A single date, a plain list of dates and a weekday phrase
take the time directly (每月1日、15日0:00), which every judge read at
once. The third judge noticed the difference; it is this rule, not an
accident.

Inside the time part, a stretch of time and what happens in it are parted
by a comma, and a frequency ends in 一次: 9:00至17:45，每15分钟一次.

* `0 9 * * *` — 每天9:00
* `*/15 9-17 * * 1-5` — 每周一至周五9:00至17:45，每15分钟一次
* `0 0 1 * *` — 每月1日0:00
* `* * * * *` — 每分钟一次

2.2 Each new part is added before the parts finer than it. Nothing moves.

* `0 9 * 6 *` — 每年6月每天9:00
* `0 9 * * 1` — 每周一9:00
* `0 9 * 6 1` — 每年6月每周一9:00

2.3 Seconds close the time, as the finest part.

* `30 0 9 * * *` — 每天9:00:30
* `*/6 0 9 * * *` — 每天9:00:00至9:00:54，每6秒一次
* `* 0 9 * * *` — 每天9:00:00至9:00:59，每秒一次

2.4 No second order was adopted. Inside the time part the order is also
coarse to fine: the stretch of hours first, then what happens in it.

2.5 每天. A time of day with only a month or a year before it skips a
level. So when the hours are restricted and neither the day of the month
nor the weekday is, the join is 每天 (每天9:00 was graded natural twice).

* `0 9 * * *` — 每天9:00
* `0 9 * 6 *` — 每年6月每天9:00
* `0 0 9 * * * 2030` — 2030年每天9:00
* `0 9-17 * * *` — 每天9:00至17:00，每小时一次
* `0 5/2 * * *` — 每天5:00至23:00，每2小时一次
* `0 1/2 * * *` — 每天奇数整点
* `* */2 * * *` — 每天偶数小时内，每分钟一次

It is not said when the time runs round the clock (2.1): every hour, or
a rhythm of hours, with or without exceptions. The time part then says
the rhythm itself. (2026-11-19: so a tiling cadence of hours has no 每
天, as on the morning of 2026-11-14.)

* `*/5 * * 6 *` — 每年6月，每5分钟一次
* `30 * * * *` — 每小时的30分
* `*/10 0-2,4-23 * * *` — 每10分钟一次（3:00至3:59除外）
* `0 */2 * * *` — 每2小时一次
* `0 */12 * * *` — 每12小时一次
* `30 */6 * * *` — 每6小时的30分

An edit to the hours may therefore change the join. So may an edit to
the minutes, in one declared case, **the rhythm of hours**: hours that
tile the day from 0:00 are a rhythm while their minutes are moments (每2
小时的30分) and hold their minutes where those are stretches (每天偶数小
时内，每分钟一次), so the minute decides whether 每天 is said. No other
part depends on the time. The time depends on the rest of the sentence
in one declared case, **the overnight stretch**: hours that run past midnight are said as
one stretch to 次日 only when nothing but the time is restricted (3.5),
so an edit that names or un-names a day, a month or a year may turn
22:00至次日2:00，每小时一次 into 0:00、1:00、2:00、22:00、23:00 and back.
It has to: 每周一22:00至次日2:00 would promise Tuesday's small hours, which
do not fire, and leave out Monday's, which do.

A second declared case is **the schedule that never fires** (4.4): where
the dates named exist in none of the months and years named, the whole
description is 永不执行（…）, and an edit to the date, the month or the
year that makes or unmakes that case changes every part.
`test/lang/zh/continuity.js` declares all three by these names and allows
nothing else to cross.

2.6 每年. A date or a month with no year before it reads as a single
occasion: 1月1日0:00 looks like one New Year. So when the month is
restricted and the year is not, the year part says 每年: 每年1月1日0:00; 每年
6月每天9:00; 每年6月至8月每周一9:00. A named year replaces it.

## 3. Devices, field by field

Numbers are Arabic digits everywhere, including after 第 (第2个周一). 一
in 一次, 一轮 and 最后一个 is part of the word, not a count.

3.1 Every value: silent, except that a time with nothing else to say is
每分钟一次, and with S every second, 每秒一次.

* `* * * * *` — 每分钟一次
* `0 * * * *` — 每小时整点

3.2 One value.

* Minute, with H every hour: 每小时的30分; `1 * * * *` is 每小时的1分.
  **Contested.** The first judge ranked 每小时的第30分钟 first and found
  30分 "a little like a duration"; the second called 第N分钟 machine talk
  and ranked 每小时的30分 first. The schedule reader settled it: an
  ordinal counts from one and the pattern counts from zero, so 每小时的第1
  分钟 is read as xx:00 as readily as xx:01, and 第30分钟 as xx:29. N分
  is how a clock is read (9点1分), names the same minute for every N, and
  cannot be one off. 的 keeps it a position; 每小时30分 with no 的 is
  never written (it reads as every 90 minutes). The third judge again
  preferred 第30分钟 (30分 can flash as a duration) and read a list of N分
  as clock positions without trouble; the decision stands on the
  off-by-one, which no judge's preference removes. The number has no leading
  zero (the first judge: 05分 is machine writing). Minute 0 with every
  hour is the idiom 每小时整点 (5.4).
* Second, with every minute: 每分钟的30秒.
* Minute with one hour: a clock time, 9:30 (4.1).
* Hour with minute 0: 9:00.
* Day of the month: 每月15日. A date some months lack is led by 每逢
  (4.4): 每逢31日.
* Month: 6月.
* Weekday: 每周一.

3.3 A list. Items are joined by the enumeration comma 、 with nothing
before the last item.

* Minutes: 每小时的15分、45分; 每小时的5分、20分、45分; 每小时的0分、15分、45
  分. Each carries its 分. Seconds the same: 每分钟的5秒、15秒.
* Hours with minute 0: 8:00、12:00、18:00.
* Days of the month: 每月1日、10日、20日. One 每月 for the list.
* Months: 1月、7月.
* Weekdays: 每周一、周三、周五. One 每 for the list. **The week runs from
  Monday to Sunday**: 每周一、周日; 每周六、周日; 每周五、周日. Only a run
  of three days or more crosses the weekend (3.5).

3.4 A range is 至 between its first and last members, and both fire.

* Hours at one minute, five or more: 9:00至17:00，每小时一次; at minute 30,
  9:30至17:30，每小时一次. The window runs from the first time that fires
  to the last.
* Days: 每月1日至7日. Months: 6月至8月. Years: 2030年至2035年. Weekdays: 每周二
  至周四 (one 每, one 周 each).
* A stretch of hours filled by a finer rhythm is a window that names its
  last moment: hours 9 to 17 under every minute are 9:00至17:59，每分钟一
  次; under every second, 9:00:00至17:59:59，每秒一次; under a cadence the
  window closes on the last run, 9:00至17:45，每15分钟一次.
* A run of minutes inside one named hour: 9:00至9:29，每分钟一次.
* A run of minutes under every hour or a stretch of hours: 每小时的0分至29
  分，每分钟一次; 每小时的45分至59分，每分钟一次. Seconds the same: 每分钟的0
  秒至9秒，每秒一次.

3.5 A range that wraps says so with 次 before the unit it crosses: 22:00至次
日2:00，每小时一次; 22:00至次日2:59，每分钟一次; 11月至次年2月.

* Hours wrap only when the day of the month, the weekday, the month and
  the year are all every value.
* Months wrap only when the year is every value, and only as a range: two
  months across the new year are a list, in the order of the year, 每年1
  月、12月 (not 12月、1月, which no rule of order gives).
* Weekdays wrap as a run of three days or more that crosses from Sunday
  to Monday, with 次周 before its last day: `5-1` is 每周五至次周周一;
  `0,1,6` is 每周六至次周周一; `0-4` is 每周日至次周周四. The run begins
  later than every other piece, so it comes last: `0,1,2,4` is 每周四、
  周日至次周周二. Two days across the weekend are a list in the
  Monday-to-Sunday week, 每周一、周日. (2026-11-19, from the comparison:
  the sister said 每週五至週一. Until then no weekday wrapped, and `5-1`
  was 每周一、周五至周日: three things, and the reader had to put Monday
  after Sunday for himself. 每周五至周一 had been dropped because Sunday
  led in one row and trailed in the next; 次周 removes that, and is how a
  mainland notice writes it: 每周三至次周周一 is quoted in the notes from
  a museum. Costs: the same ten syllables; two things against three; no
  re-ordering to do.)
* Days of the month wrap only where no month is named, as a run of three
  dates or more across the month's end, with 次月: `1-10,14-31` is 每月
  14日至次月10日每天; `28-3` is 每月28日至次月3日每天; among pieces it
  comes last, 每月10日、20日至次月5日的9:00. One or two dates missing are
  still an exception (3.9: 每月除15日外每天). Under a named month the
  dates stay in the order of the month (6月1日至10日、14日至30日): 至次月
  would leave the months named. (2026-11-19, from the comparison. It was
  每月1日至10日、14日至31日的9:00, two stretches for what is one, as a
  billing period is written, 每月26日至次月25日. Costs: 14 syllables
  against 18, two things against four.)
* Minutes and seconds follow 3.6.

Where a wrap is not allowed, the set is described in ascending order:
`0 22-2 1 * *` is five times and is listed, 每月1日0:00、1:00、2:00、22:00、
23:00; `*/15 22-2 * * 1-5` is 每周一至周五0:00至2:45、22:00至23:45，每15分钟
一次; `0 0 * 11-2 * 2030` is 2030年1月、2月、11月、12月每天0:00.

3.6 Pieces.

* Dates, months, weekdays and years: a list in which an item may be a
  range, joined by 、 only. 每月1日至7日、15日; 每月1日至5日、10日、20日至25
  日; 6月至8月、12月; 每周二至周五、周日; 每周一、周三至周五; 2030年至2032
  年、2035年. A run of three or more inside the list is a range; shorter
  runs are named.
* Two or more windows of time with the same rhythm are a list of windows
  with the rhythm said once: 9:00至17:59、22:00至22:59，每分钟一次; 9:00至
  17:45、22:00至22:45，每15分钟一次.
* A window and stray points: the window with its rhythm, then 以及 and the
  points. 9:00至17:00，每小时一次，以及22:00; 9:00至17:00，每小时一次，以及
  20:00、22:00; 9:00至9:29，每分钟一次，以及9:45; 每小时的0分至29分，每分钟一
  次，以及45分. **What follows 以及 carries its own hour wherever the hours
  are restricted** (4.1): after the comma it no longer hangs on a 的
  before it, so a bare 45分 there is minute 45 of every hour.

Pieces are said in ascending order of their first value, with no
exception. A run that wraps begins later than every other piece, so it
comes last, where the day or the year turns: 每天9:00至17:00、22:00至次日
2:00，每小时一次; 每天10:00至10:59、22:00至次日2:59，每分钟一次; 每年6月、
11月至次年2月. (The wrapped run was first said first. The audit turned it
round: 每天22:00至次日2:00、9:00至17:00 puts a morning after 次日, and a
reader has to decide which day it belongs to.) After a window with its
rhythm, stray points follow 以及 whatever their hour: 每天6:00至23:00，每小
时一次，以及2:00、3:00、4:00.

A run of minutes that wraps inside one hour is two windows: 9:00至9:10、
9:50至9:59，每分钟一次.

3.7 An even cadence.

* Minutes under every hour, tiling from 0: 每15分钟一次; minutes 0 and 30
  are 每30分钟一次. Seconds: 每15秒一次.
* **Hours that tile the day from 0:00 are a rhythm, said by the step**
  (2026-11-19): every second, third, fourth, sixth, eighth or twelfth
  hour, from 0. The step stands where 每小时 would, and what would follow
  每小时 follows it, with one difference: at minute 0 it ends in 一次
  where every hour has 整点.

  | M (and S) | Every hour | A rhythm of hours |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | minute 0 | 每小时整点 | 每2小时一次; 每3小时一次; 每6小时一次; 每12小时一次 |
  | one minute | 每小时的30分 | 每2小时的30分; 每6小时的23分 |
  | a list of minutes | 每小时的5分、35分 | 每2小时的5分、35分; 每3小时的5分、35分; 每6小时的15分、45分 |
  | with seconds (4.2, case 5) | 每小时的0分15秒; 每小时的30分0秒至30分50秒，每10秒一次 | 每2小时的0分15秒; 每3小时的24分0秒至24分50秒，每10秒一次; 每2小时的5分、20分各一轮，… |

  It is a rhythm only while its minutes are **moments**: one minute, or
  minutes that are listed. Under a step longer than two a cadence of
  eight minutes or fewer is its minutes, so it is a rhythm too: 每6小时
  的0分、15分、30分、45分. Where the minutes are a **stretch** (every
  minute, a run, a cadence said by its ends, or, under every second
  hour, a cadence that tiles the hour) the hours hold them, and are said
  as hours that hold minutes are (4.1): the even hours by their idiom
  (5.5), 每天偶数小时内，每15分钟一次, 每天偶数小时的0分至29分，每分钟一
  次; a longer step by its hours, 每天0点、6点、12点、18点的每一分钟. (每2
  小时的0分至29分 has no natural reading: it is the stretch that needs an
  hour to be in.)

  The rhythm runs round the clock, so it takes no 每天 and follows a day
  after a comma (2.1): 每月1日，每2小时一次; 每周一，每2小时的30分; 每年6
  月，每3小时一次; 每月1日以及每周五，均为每2小时一次. It says the same
  under a named day as alone. However it was written, the same hours are
  the same rhythm: `0 0,12 * * *` is 每12小时一次.

  **The odd hours are not a rhythm** (they do not start at 0:00) and keep
  their idiom everywhere (5.5): 每天奇数整点; 每天奇数小时的15分、45分;
  每天奇数小时内，每分钟一次. The comparison preferred this to the
  sister's stated start (每天 01:00 起每2小時一次): the word names the
  hours, and nothing is added up. So this language calls the two halves
  of the day's hours 偶数小时 and 奇数小时, never 单数 or 双数, and the
  even hours are named that way wherever they are not a rhythm.

  Any other cadence of hours off 0:00 that starts again the next day
  where it started (a first hour within one step of 0:00), with more
  than four times, is said from its first time with 每天: 每天1:00起每3
  小时一次. Under a named date or weekday 起 can run on past that day, so
  there the cadence keeps both ends: 每周一2:00至23:00，每3小时一次. Four
  times or fewer are listed; so are up to eight that are no such cadence
  (每天3:00、6:00、9:00、12:00、15:00、18:00、21:00).

  Why (2026-11-19). The lead was the comparison: for `0 */3 * * *` the
  sister says 每3小時一次 and this language said 每天0:00起每3小时一次,
  and for the one kind it had four forms: 每天偶数整点 (step 2), a stated
  start (steps 3 and 4), a window (those under a named day) and a list
  (steps 6, 8, 12). All four came from the second and third amendments of
  2026-11-14, on two model readers who read 每2小时一次 as counted from
  when the task starts, as a dose is timed. That reading is real for a
  medicine label and is exactly what README "Description Accuracy" says
  the library does not qualify; it holds in every language, and readers
  do not reopen it. Compared by hand (syllables as spoken; things held;
  what is said twice; what is worked out):

  | Pattern | Before | After | Verdict |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `0 */2 * * *` | 每天偶数整点: 6; 1; 0; none | 每2小时一次: 6; 1; 0; none (the convention) | level; the step is the form every other step takes |
  | `0 */3 * * *` | 每天0:00起每3小时一次: 11; 2; 0; adding on from a start | 每3小时一次: 6; 1; 0; none | better in every column |
  | `0 */6 * * *` | 每天0:00、6:00、12:00、18:00: 12; 4; 0; none | 每6小时一次: 6; 1; 0; none | better or level in every column |
  | `0 */4 1 * *` | 每月1日0:00至20:00，每4小时一次: 16; 4; 0; whether 20:00 is the last | 每月1日，每4小时一次: 10; 2; 0; none | better in every column |
  | `30 */2 * * *` | 每天偶数小时的30分: 10; 2; 0; none | 每2小时的30分: 8; 2; 0; none | level; one form with minute 0 |
  | `5,35 */3 * * *` | 每天0点、3点、6点、9点、12点、15点、18点、21点的5分、35分: 30; 10; 0; none | 每3小时的5分、35分: 11; 3; 0; none | better in every column |
  | `15,45 */6 * * *` | 每天0:15、0:45、6:15、6:45、12:15、12:45、18:15、18:45: 50; 8; each hour twice; none | 每6小时的15分、45分: 12; 3; 0; none | better in every column |
  | `0 */12 * * *` | 每天0:00、12:00: 7; 2; 0; none | 每12小时一次: 7; 1; 0; none | level: two exact times are as easy; the step is said so that the kind has one form and the spelling is never followed |

  Rejected: 每天偶数小时的30分 for the even hours under a minute, though
  it reads well: it would be a second form for one kind, and an edit of
  the minute from 0 to 30 would turn 每2小时 into 偶数小时. Rejected: the
  stated start under a minute (the sister's 每天 00:30 起每2小時一次): a
  start to add to, where 每2小时的30分 is read as 每小时的30分 is. 每2小
  时的30分 is plain but not found in a source; it is doubted most at the
  longest steps, where 每12小时的15分、45分 stands for four clock times
  that would be as easy to read.
* **Any other cadence of hours keeps both ends**: hours 5 to 23, every
  second, run 5:00至23:00 and leave six hours overnight. At one minute:
  每天5:00至23:00，每2小时一次; 每天0:00至20:00，每2小时一次. Under other
  minutes the even or odd hours after the stretch from their first
  firing to their last (5.5): 每天3:05至23:35，奇数小时的5分、35分.
* Days of the month never tile. Up to eight dates are listed: `*/5` is 每
  月1日、6日、11日、16日、21日、26日、31日. Every second day of the whole
  month is an idiom (5.1). **Every second date between two ends, more
  than eight, is the odd or the even days from its first date to its
  last**: `8/2` is 每月8日至30日逢双日; `1-17/2` is 每月1日至17日逢单日;
  `5/2` is 每月5日至31日逢单日; in June `1-27/2` is 6月1日至27日逢单日.
  Where the dates reach the last odd or even day of the months named, the
  month is their end, and they are the idiom (3.11): in June `1-29/2` is
  6月的单日.
  **Any other cadence is listed, however many**: `*/3` is 每月1日、4日、7
  日、10日、13日、16日、19日、22日、25日、28日、31日 (eleven dates, the
  most there can be).
  (2026-11-19, from the comparison. These were the first three dates,
  ……, and the last: 每月1日、4日、7日……31日; 每月8日、10日、12日……30日. A
  series that leaves the step to be worked out is what `CLAUDE.md` rules
  out: the reader has to find the step and then trust that it holds to
  the end. 单日 and 双日 are this language's own words (5.1, and the
  notes: 单日／双日); between two ends they need only 至, so the form is
  the sister's 8日至30日的雙數日 in this language's words, not a loan.
  They follow 逢, as in 每月逢单日, and 的 only where 每逢 already leads
  the dates (每逢8日至30日的双日以及每逢周一). The first form written was
  每月5日至31日的单日: in the reading check two of three readers, asked
  in a list, took an even date to fire on three such rows, as if 单日
  were "each single day"; alone, readers of two models read it rightly.
  With 逢 the fixed phrase 逢单日 is kept whole; asked alone about three
  dates, three Sonnet readers and two of three Opus readers read 每月5日
  至31日逢单日 rightly each time.
  For every third date no word exists. 每月1日起每3天一次 was weighed
  and rejected: the day part would end in a rate, and the frame has no
  join from a rate to a clock time; under a named month 起 runs on past
  the month; and 每月1日至31日每3天一次 had been dropped before, since a
  reader does not see that the count restarts on the 1st. 每隔 is not
  used (the notes: 每隔两天 is every second day to some and every third
  to others). Costs for `*/3`: 14 syllables against 37; four things and
  a step to infer against eleven things and nothing to infer. The
  columns disagree, and the list was chosen: it is exact, each date is
  recognised, and a reader who wants to know whether the 22nd fires can
  see it. For `8/2`: 14 syllables against 13, four things and a step to
  infer against three and none: better in every column. `5/2`
  and `1-29/2` were 每月逢单日（1日、3日除外） and 每月逢单日（31日除
  外）: three or four things and a subtraction against three and none.)
* Weekdays have no cadence, and are named. Every second month is the odd
  or the even months (5.5): `*/2` is 奇数月. Any other months are named:
  `*/3` is 1月、4月、7月、10月.
* Years: see 5.8.

3.8 A cadence that does not divide evenly, or starts off the beat.

* Up to eight values: listed. 每小时的5分、20分、35分、50分; 每小时的5分、15
  分、25分、35分、45分、55分; 每天0:00、5:00、10:00、15:00、20:00.
* More than eight: both ends. 每天5:00至23:00，每2小时一次; 每小时的0分至56
  分，每7分钟一次; inside one named hour, a window of clock times, 9:00至
  9:56，每7分钟一次.
* **More than eight clock times, minutes or seconds that are several
  stretches, each with its own step** (and at most a few strays): each
  stretch is followed directly by its rhythm, with no comma inside it,
  and the stretches are parted by commas, strays last after 以及. 每小时的
  0分至20分每2分钟一次，21分至57分每3分钟一次; 每天0:00至12:00每2小时一
  次，13:00至22:00每3小时一次; 每天9:00至9:20每2分钟一次，9:21至9:57每3分
  钟一次; 每分钟的0秒至20秒每2秒一次，21秒至57秒每3秒一次. It is said only
  when the set is no single cadence and takes no exception (3.9), and
  when it names fewer things than the runs of five or more and the other
  values one by one would (a stretch is one thing, a lone value one).
  The stretches are those the core finds (`describeCadencePieces`); the
  renderer does not look for them itself. `*/2,43-7` is 每小时的0分至8分每
  分钟一次，10分至40分每2分钟一次，42分至59分每分钟一次. Where the core
  finds one cadence and stray values, the steps do not differ and the
  usual form of 3.6 is said: 每天9:00:01至9:00:15，每2秒一次，以及9:00:18;
  每天0:00至22:00，每2小时一次，以及13:00. The form is that of a timetable (高峰15分
  钟一班，平峰30分钟一班, from the notes): parallel clauses, each whole.
  The comma that elsewhere parts a window from its rhythm is dropped here
  because it would be the same comma that parts the clauses. (Until the
  second repair such a set was a list of up to 24 minutes.) Dates do not
  take the form: a month holds few enough that the list stays short.

3.9 A set with values missing is said as the simpler set with the missing
ones excepted, when the table at the top allows it and the exception
names fewer things than the list.

**A single run is always a range, never an exception.** Months 1 to 11 are
每年1月至11月; Monday to Saturday is 每周一至周六; Tuesday to Sunday is 每周
二至周日. An exception is for a hole in the middle.

**When an exception is said in the time part.** Hours, minutes and seconds
take one only when all of these hold; otherwise the set is its pieces or
its list.

* More than eight values fire; or the set is **a rhythm with a beat or
  two missing** (2026-11-19): a cadence that tiles its field from the
  first value (hours of the day from 0:00, minutes of the hour or seconds
  of the minute from 0), with one or two values missing between values
  that fire and more than four left. The rhythm is said, and what is
  missing follows in the bracket: 每2小时一次（8:00除外）; 每3小时一次
  （12:00除外）; 每4小时的30分（8:30除外）; 每2小时的15分、45分（8:00至
  8:59除外）; 每10分钟一次（每小时的30分除外）; 每天9:00至17:50，每10分
  钟一次（每小时的30分除外）; at one clock time, 每天9:00:00至9:00:50，
  每10秒一次（9:00:30除外）. It is said only where the rhythm itself is:
  hours, where they are then a rhythm of hours (3.7); minutes, where a
  tiling cadence is said by its step (not under hours named one by one,
  and not where the clock times are eight or fewer, which are listed: 每
  天9:00、9:10、9:20、9:40、9:50); seconds, at a single clock time. The
  odd hours take the same exception when more than eight are left: 每天
  奇数整点（7:00除外）; and the even hours under minutes that are
  stretches, 每天偶数小时内，每分钟一次（8:00至8:59除外）.
  (2026-11-19, from the comparison: the sister says 每10分鐘一次（每小時
  的30分除外） where this language listed 每小时的0分、10分、20分、40分、
  50分, and 每3小時一次（12:00 除外） where it listed seven clock times.
  A rule with one exception is something people hold well; five or seven
  things in a list are more than the four a reader keeps in mind. Costs
  for the minutes: 15 syllables against 17, two things and a
  subtraction against five things. The columns disagree on the
  subtraction; with five or more left the rule was judged the easier to
  hold, and with four or fewer the list is. 2026-11-14: such hours were
  the window of 3.8 with the bracket, then the bare step with the
  bracket, then the even hours' idiom with it.)
* What is left when the holes are filled is every value, or an even
  cadence (for whole hours under minutes that are not one minute: every
  hour, or the hours of 5.5). Never a mere range: 每小时的0分至44分 has
  nothing excepted.
* Every hole lies between two values that fire. A hole at an end is a
  later start or an earlier finish, and is said as one: 每天1:00至23:00，每
  小时一次; 每小时的0分至58分，每分钟一次.
* It names fewer things than the pieces would. Count each excepted value
  as one, and the two ends of a cadence that names its ends; against
  that, each run of the set as it stands counts its two ends and each
  lone value one. Minutes 0 to 9 and 11 to 59 are one against four: 每分钟
  一次（每小时的10分除外）. Minutes 0 to 20, 25 and 30 to 50 are many
  against five, and stay 每小时的0分至20分、30分至50分，每分钟一次，以及25
  分.
* It names fewer things than the set would as stretches each with its
  own step (3.8), where the core finds such stretches, two or more (one
  cadence with stray values is not such a set: 3.8): there a stretch
  counts its two ends. `*/2,43-7` would except seventeen minutes, and is
  three stretches instead.
* **Hours that cross midnight are counted as the two runs they are inside
  one day**, so the choice never turns on whether a day is named. With
  one gap in the day, one to three hours missing are an exception, 每小时整点（9:00除
  外）, 每小时整点（5:00、6:00、7:00除外）, with or without a day; four or
  more leave an overnight stretch, 每天10:00至次日5:00，每小时一次, or
  beside a day its two pieces, 每周一0:00至5:00、10:00至23:00，每小时一次.
  (每天10:00至次日8:00，每小时一次 for "every hour but 9:00" was rejected:
  it makes the reader walk round the clock to find one missing hour.)

Of the exceptions the arithmetic offers, the first that passes is said:
from every value before from a cadence, and a cadence that spans the
values before one stretched to the ends of the field.

* Dates, more than eight with one or two missing: 每月除15日外每天; 每月除15
  日、16日外每天; in one month, 6月除15日外每天. On the odd or even days:
  每月逢单日（15日除外）. The missing dates may fall anywhere, the first
  and the last included, so long as what is left is not one run: 每月除1
  日、15日外每天. (A date is not the end of a window, so the reason that
  keeps a hole off the ends of a stretch of time does not apply.) From
  the odd or even days only a date between two that fire is excepted;

## 5. Idioms

5.1 Days of the month 1, 3, 5 … 31: 每月逢单日. Days 2, 4 … 30: 每月逢双
日. Each can take exceptions (3.9). After a named month or a list of
months 逢 gives way to 的: 6月的单日; 1月、7月的单日 (4.4). Both judges
graded 逢单日 natural. Fifteen or sixteen dates cannot be listed. More
than eight of them between two ends are 逢单日 or 逢双日 after the ends
(3.7): 每月8日至30日逢双日; 每月1日至17日逢单日.

5.2 None. Monday to Friday is the range 每周一至周五 and the weekend is the
list 每周六、周日. 工作日 is not used anywhere, with or without a gloss: in
mainland China working days are moved around public holidays (调休), so a
Sunday can be a 工作日 and a Friday not, and the pattern knows no
holidays. 周末 is not used either (it can take in Friday evening).

5.3 None. Noon and midnight are 12:00 and 0:00. 午夜 "不能用": 1日午夜 is
read as the end of the 1st.

5.4 Minute 0 with every hour is 每小时整点. Once a second is said it is a
minute like any other, 每小时的0分30秒 (4.2).

  (`31W`; `30W` in June): a Sunday has no Monday after it, and both days
  move back, 每逢31日（遇周六、周日提前至周五）; 每年6月30日（遇周六、周
  日提前至周五）. Sometimes (`28W`, `29W`, `30W` under every month): 每
  月28日（遇周六提前至周五；遇周日顺延至周一，如当天是当月最后一天则提前
  至周五）; a month without the date is skipped, 每逢30日（…）(4.4).
  (2026-11-19, from the comparison. It was （遇周六、周日改在当月最近的
  周五或周一） for all of these: it left the reader to work out which of
  the two, and "或" reads as a choice; the sister says which way each
  day moves. 提前 and 顺延 are the notes' fixed words, and already this
  language's for `LW` and `1W`. Costs: 23 syllables against 24, and
  nothing to work out where there was a nearest day to find. The long
  form for the 28th to the 30th is 40 syllables against 25, the one
  change here that is longer: it is exact, where the short one covered a
  Sunday that ends its month only by the word 当月.)
* `1#2` — 每月第2个周一
* `5L` — 每月最后一个周五

工作日 was said for `LW` and `1W` until the second repair, glossed （周一
至周五）. The gloss cannot rescue the word: by the notes a reader of 工作
日 expects the Spring Festival to be skipped and a make-up Saturday to
count, and a gloss that contradicts its headword leaves two readings on
the screen. The forms now name the date and say which way it moves, with
the fixed words for that: 提前 (earlier) and 顺延 (later, never earlier).
They are exact: the last day moves back to Friday from either weekend
day, the 1st moves on to Monday from either.

5.8 Years.

* All even years (a cadence of two that starts at the first year there
  is): 每逢偶数年; all odd years: 每逢奇数年. 每逢 marks the recurrence,
  as 每 does everywhere else.
* Any other cadence of up to eight years is listed: 2030年、2032年、2034
  年、2036年、2038年、2040年.
* A cadence with no end names its start and its step, and which year of
  each step fires, the first: 自2028年起每4年中的第1年，当年…; 自2030年起每2
  年中的第1年，当年…. It differs from 每逢偶数年 because it has a first year.
* A cadence of more than eight years that ends names both ends: 2030年至
  2060年每2年中的第1年，当年….

* Every year from one on, with no end, is 自2030年起: 自2030年起每天9:00;
  自2030年起，每5分钟一次. (It was 2030年至9999年, the last year the
  pattern can hold, which no one would write.) The core marks a run or a
  cadence that has no end; the renderer never compares a year with the
  last one there is.

More than eight years that are none of these take the first that applies:

1. **One or two years missing** from every year, from a run, or from a
   cadence. From every year: 除2030年外 (除2030年外每天9:00; 除2030年外每年
   1月1日0:00; 除2030年外，每5分钟一次). From a run, a bracket after it:
   2030年至2052年（2051年除外）每天9:00. From the even or odd years: 每逢偶数
   年（2030年除外）每天9:00. From a cadence, a bracket after it: 2000年至
   2098年每2年中的第1年（2030年除外），当年每天9:00.
2. **Up to eight runs and lone years**: a list, as in 3.6, 2030年、2032
   年、2040年至2060年每天9:00. A run with no end comes last, after 以及:
   2025年以及自2030年起每天9:00. (The audit left 2025年、自2030年起, where
   the 、 makes 自 look like the start of a new sentence.)
3. **A few lone years and then one run or cadence**: the fewest leading
   years, up to eight, that leave a single run or cadence. The lone years
   are listed, then 以及 and the rest as it would be said alone: 1984年以及
   自2042年起每2年中的第1年，当年每天9:00; 2030
   年、2031年以及自2032年起每2年中的第1年，当年
   每天9:00.
4. **Runs and cadences together**, as the core gives them: each said as
   it would be alone (a cadence of up to eight years that ends is its
   years), in the order of their first years. They are a list with 、;
   where any has no end or is a cadence said with its rule, the last is
   joined by 以及 instead, and such a cadence puts 当年 after the whole.
   2030年至2040年、2050年、2052年、2054年、2056年、2058年、2060年、2062年、
   2070年至2080年每天9:00; 2030年至2040年、2050年至2090年每2年中的第1年
   以及2100年至2110年，当年每天9:00. 2030年
   至2040年以及自2045年起每5年中的第1年，当年每
   天9:00; 自2030年起每3年中的第1年以及2040
   年、2050年，当年每天9:00. (These were lists of thousands of years until
   the second repair.)
5. Otherwise the list, however long.

Case 3 stands before case 4 because the stretches the core finds for a
lone year before a cadence are not the ones a reader would (it takes the
lone year as the start of a long cadence of its own and cuts the real one
into hundreds). With that mended in the core, case 3 could go.

**Contested.** The first judge found 2028年起每4年，每天9:00 odd (two
frequencies side by side) and ranked the years with …… first. The second
found that the years alone make the reader guess the rule and think of
leap years. The third took 每4年一次 for one run in four years until 当年每
天 overturned it. So the rule was said with the first years in a bracket:
自2028年起每4年中的一年（2028年、2032年、2036年……）. (2026-11-14: the
bracket is gone. It showed a series of years to illustrate what the words
already said; the one thing only it said, which year of each four, is
now said in words, 第1年. 每4年中的第1年 cannot be one run, and 当年 keeps
the two frequencies apart.) 每隔 is never used: 每隔4年 is every fifth
year.

## 6. Words

6.1 Syllables, which are characters; a digit string is read out.

6.2 Numbers are Arabic digits. The minutes and seconds of a clock time
are two digits (9:05); a minute or second named by its number is not (5
分, 8秒).

6.3 Months are the digit and 月. Weekdays are 周一 to 周六 and 周日.

6.4 秒, 分钟 (分 after the number of a minute in the hour), 小时 (点 after
the number of an hour before 的…分), 天, 日 (after a date's number), 月,
年. There are no plural forms.

6.5 每, 至, 以及, 及, 的, 内, 其中, 次, 次日, 次周, 次月, 次年, 当年, 各月,
除, 外, 除外, 整点, 所在分钟的, 一次, 一轮, 各一轮, 每轮, 间隔, 首次在, 在,
逢, 每逢, 自, 起, 中的第1年, 偶数小时, 奇数小时, 奇数整点, 偶数月, 奇数
月, 遇, 为, 时, 或, 照常, 提前至, 顺延至, 如当天是当月最后一天则, 永不执行, 没
有, 当月无此日则不执行, 仅闰年, 每逢闰年.

## 7. Agreement

7.1 None. Nouns do not change with number.

7.2 None.

7.3 None.

7.4 A list: 、 between items, nothing before the last. A window and stray
points: 以及. A date and a weekday together: 以及. Runs and cadences of
years together: 以及. Two exceptions together: 及.

7.5 Units follow their numbers directly, with no space: 15分钟, 1日, 9:00
至17:45.

7.6 Left to right. Arabic digits and the colon of a clock time are
half-width; punctuation is full-width. No spaces anywhere, including
between digits and characters.

## 8. Traps

8.1 `union-connective` — 以及, each side with its own 每月, 每周 or 每逢,
and ，均在 before the time: 每月1日以及每周五，均在0:00; ，均为 before a
rate: 每月1日以及每周五，均为每分钟一次.

8.2 `shared-qualifier-scope` — 均 after the pair makes the time belong to
both sides, where 的 could take the last side alone. A month is said
once, before a comma, and 每逢 leads each side: 每年6月，每逢1日以及每逢周
五，均在0:00.

8.3 `confinement-vs-juxtaposition` — A confinement is a window before the
rhythm, parted from it by a comma (9:00至17:59，每分钟一次; 9:00:00至
9:00:59，每秒一次), or 一轮 and the count of each round (每轮60次).

8.4 `redundancy` — 每月 is not said after a named month (6月1日). 每分钟 is
not said beside 每秒. A field that takes every value is not said.

8.5 `range-boundary` — Every range of times is closed and names its last
moment, to the second where seconds fire. Outside an exception bracket,
no clock time is named that does not fire: a stretch of hours before
named minutes opens on its first firing and closes on its last (4.1). No
date is named that the months named do not have (4.4), and
no hour or end of a window is named that a bracket then takes back.

8.6 `recurrence-marking` — 每周 before a weekday, once for a list or a
range; 每月 before a date; 每天; 每逢 before 偶数年.

8.7 `numeral-register` — Arabic digits for every count and position.

8.8 `sentence-wrapper-punctuation` — Chinese has no capitals: a label is
the description as it stands. A sentence is 执行时间： followed by the
description and a full stop: 执行时间：每天9:00。 执行时间：每5分钟一次。
(The description followed by 执行 is not Chinese after 一次.) The
description for `@reboot` is 系统启动时. The lenient fallback is 无法识别
的cron表达式, which is never wrapped.

8.9 `cardinality-rendering` — Decided by the table at the top. `0 0 1
*/3 *` is 每年1月、4月、7月、10月的1日0:00; `0 0 1 */6 *` is 每年1月、7月的1
日0:00; `0 */6 * * *` and `0 0,6,12,18 * * *` are 每6小时一次; `0 */8 * *
*` is 每8小时一次; `0 */12 * * *` is 每12小时一次 (3.7; 2026-11-19).

## 9. Where this rule book departs from the usage notes

The notes (`usage.md`) were written after this book, by a writer told
nothing of schedulers. The second repair went through the book beside
them. First what was taken from them, then each place the book still
differs, with its reason. The notes mark what is quoted from a source
(出处), what is the writer's own judgment (判断) and what is from memory
(记忆); a departure from a judgment weighs less than one from a source.

**Taken from the notes.**

* 工作日 is gone (5.2, 5.7). The notes show from the State Council's
  holiday notices and a Beijing traffic order that it is an institutional
  word: a make-up Sunday is a 工作日 and a holiday Friday is not. `LW` and
  `1W` now name the date and which way it moves, with 提前 and 顺延, the
  notes' fixed words for a date moved off a rest day.
* 和 between a date and a weekday became 以及, each side with its own
  mark of recurrence (4.3): the notes' warning about 每月13日和周五.
* A date that some months lack said so in the notes' own words, 每月31日
  （当月无此日则不执行）, until 2026-11-19; it is now 每逢31日, on the
  notes' account of 逢 (4.4). The words remain for a day counted back
  from the month's end.
* Weekdays across the weekend are 每周五至次周周一 (3.5), the notes'
  sourced 每周三至次周周一.
* 照常 after an exception (4.3) says positively what happens on the
  day, as the notes recommend.
* 整分 and the "past" constructions (整点过30秒, 整分过15秒) are gone (4.2):
  the notes call the first a coinage and list the second among forms
  carried over from English.
* 早上 for the hours 5 to 7 on the 12-hour clock (1.2).
* Stretches that each have their own rhythm are parallel clauses, as in
  the timetables the notes quote (3.8).

**Kept against the notes.**

* **The time follows the days with 的, nothing, or ，均在; never a bare
  comma.** The notes write 每月13日，以及每周五，12:00. Here it is 每月13日
  以及每周五，均在12:00. A bare comma before the time would meet the comma
  inside the time part (9:00至17:45，每15分钟一次), and the reader could
  not tell which parts the rhythm belongs to; 均在 says that the time
  belongs to both sides and opens the time part. (判断 in the notes.)
* **No space between the day and the time.** The notes' examples have one
  (每天 8:30) and say themselves that spacing is a house style on which
  the standard is silent.
* **至, not a dash.** The notes allow 至 as the written form and show
  three dashes in use, one of them a typesetting accident (the character
  一). 至 cannot be mistaken for a minus, a hyphen or a digit.
* **Each weekday and date is written whole**: 每周一、周三、周五 and 每月1
  日、15日, where the notes also allow 每周一、三、五 and 每月1、15日. With
  a range in the list (每周一、周三至周五) the short form does not work, and
  one rule for every list is worth two characters. (判断 in the notes.)
* **A window says its last firing by its end, not by a note.** The notes
  hold that 9:00至17:00每小时一次 leaves the 17:00 in doubt and recommend
  第一次9:00，最后一次17:00. Here every window ends on a firing, and the
  ends that are not round (17:45, 17:59, 9:56) show it. The notes' device
  was said as a bracket, （首次…，末次…）, after a range of hours by name
  until 2026-11-19; the range is now itself the first and last firing
  (4.1), so nothing is left for the bracket to say. Still doubted for a
  single window that ends on the hour.
* **第 is not used for minutes and seconds.** The notes write 每分钟的第30
  秒. An ordinal counts from one and the clock from zero (3.2); 每分钟的30
  秒 is the clock reading. (判断 in the notes, whose own table gives 每小
  时的30分 for the minute.)
* **单月 and 双月 are not used** (5.5), though the notes list them as
  words the language has: 单月 can be "a single month" and 双月 "every two
  months" (双月刊, which the notes also cite). Every second month is 奇数
  月 or 偶数月, which have neither reading (2026-11-19; until then the
  six months were named). 单日 and 双日 are used (5.1), as in the notes.
* **偶数年, not 双数年.** The notes offer 逢双数年 (判断). Either is
  understood; 偶数年 goes with 偶数小时.
* **Parts of the day are not used on the 24-hour clock**, and 整点 is the
  only one of 整点, 半点 and 一刻 that is: 每小时的30分 serves every minute
  alike, where 半点 and 一刻 would serve two, and the choice may not turn
  on which value is named.
* **旬, 月初, 月底, 季度** are not used: the notes themselves say they are
  for a rough statement, and 每月最后一天 is what is exact.
* **共N天 after a range, （含）, and the weekday in brackets after a
  date** are devices for single occasions; a recurring 1日至7日 has no
  weekday to give, and no reader the notes cite doubts that a range of
  dates includes both ends.
* **Exceptions are said with 除外 or 除…外**, not by saying positively
  what happens on the excepted day (周日不营业): a schedule has nothing to
  say of a time it does not fire at, other than that it does not.
* **每 is said at each level it recurs on** (每年6月每周一9:00), where the
  notes' rule of thumb is one 每 on the largest unit. Their own example
  每年3月至5月，每周日 6:00 has two, and without the second the weekday
  reads as a single one.
