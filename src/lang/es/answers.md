# Spanish: answers to the language questionnaire

Answered by the orchestrating model (Claude Fable 5.2), 2026-11-12, for
Spanish as written in Spain by default, with a Mexican and United States
variant for the clock. Every example is what a careful writer would put on
a screen. Reasons are given, with the alternative that was rejected, so a
fluent reviewer can overturn a call.

These answers are a third version. The first put the time before the
day and leaned on arithmetic words («las horas pares», «los meses
impares», «cada 12 horas», «un año de cada 4»). A fluent reader judged
forty-five of its sentences without knowing how they were made, and the
second version followed. A second fluent reader then judged fifty-six of
its sentences the same way, and a third reader worked every row against
its pattern. Where a decision below rests on those readings it says
**(reader 1)**, **(reader 2)** or **(schedule reader)**, with what was
understood or stumbled on. Rewrites were taken as suggestions: the
readers did not know that every sentence has to follow from general
rules, or that one fixed order helps someone compare two schedules.

Two repairs followed, both on 2026-11-13. The first read every kind of
sentence against the times its schedule fires. The second had three
things the first did not: the core now states calendar facts (which
months a day of the month fires in, whether a schedule fires at all,
which years have no end); a list of traps found in eight languages; and
usage notes for Spanish, written in Spanish by a writer told nothing of
schedules (`usage.md`). Where a rule below was changed by the second
repair it says so, with the form it replaced.

Shorthand: **M** is the set of minutes, **H** the set of hours, **S** the
set of seconds.

Four things run through everything:

* **The frame goes from the calendar to the clock**: when in the year,
  which days, then at what time (2.1).
* **A field that takes every value is silent**, with one exception: when
  the hours are restricted and no day is named, the day part says «todos
  los días» (2.5).
* **A series is never left without an anchor.** A reader cannot tell when
  «cada 2 horas» or «cada 12 horas» lands, or whether 8:00 is in it
  **(reader 1:** "no dice desde qué hora se cuenta"; "el único defecto que
  afecta al sentido"**)**. So a cadence is said bare only where the unit
  above it anchors it for every speaker: minutes on the hour («cada 15
  minutos») and seconds on the minute («cada 15 segundos»). A cadence of
  hours, days or years is listed, or names both its ends.
* **Calendar words before arithmetic words.** A speaker lists, or uses the
  words of the clock and the calendar; nobody knows a month or an hour by
  whether its number is odd **(reader 1)**. Parity is said only of things
  a speaker knows by their number: the days of the month, years, and the
  minutes and seconds of 3.7.
* **A long series is shown.** Years: the first three, «y así
  sucesivamente», and the last where there is one (5.8). Hours filled by
  minutes: the rule, then the first two stretches and the last moment in
  brackets (4.1).
* **A word means to its readers what the pattern means, or it is not
  used.** The usage notes (`usage.md`, written in Spanish by a writer told
  nothing of schedules) show that «día laborable» may count Saturday and
  that «día hábil» is set by each country's law; both leave out public
  holidays. The pattern's Monday-to-Friday forms mean those five days,
  holidays included, so they are said by naming the days (5.7). The last
  section lists where this rule book departs from the notes, and why.
* **Stiffness on a rare schedule is accepted; a reading that fires at
  another time is not.** A third fluent reader **(reader 3)** judged
  fifty-six sentences of the third version, and only what that reader
  understood wrongly, in two ways, or on a second reading was changed.

## How a form is chosen

A sentence is easy when it needs little working out and little holding
in mind. Exact times need no working out; a cadence between two named
ends needs a little; an exception needs a subtraction. A list needs none,
but every item has to be held.

These are the judgments, as counts. None depends on which values are
named, only on how many there are and how they are arranged.

* **Seven is as long as a list of plain values gets when a cadence could
  replace it.** A cadence of seven values or fewer that is not bare is
  listed: «los días 1, 6, 11, 16, 21, 26 y 31 de cada mes»; «a las 0:00,
  4:00, 8:00, 12:00, 16:00 y 20:00»; «en los minutos 5, 15, 25, 35, 45 y
  55 de cada hora». From eight values it is a cadence with both ends:
  «cada 3 horas de 0:00 a 21:00». **(Reader 1:** seven days listed were
  preferred to «cada 5 días desde el día 1», where "el lector tiene que
  hacer la cuenta y no sabe qué pasa con el 31"; eight clock times listed
  were marked down, "el lector tiene que descubrir él el patrón"**.)**
* **Inside a named hour, a cadence of four times or more is a window of
  clock times**, «cada 15 minutos de 9:00 a 9:45», and three or fewer are
  listed, «a las 9:00, 9:20 y 9:40». The ends of such a window are clock
  times and need no working out, so the limit is lower than seven.
* **A list of clock times goes up to twelve** where nothing shorter says
  the same (4.1), **and to seven beside several seconds named after it**
  (4.1).
* **Up to four separate hours filled by minutes are each a window.**
  Beyond four, a cadence of hours is said by its rule with the stretches
  in brackets, and hours with no cadence are named by when they begin
  (4.1).
* **A run of exactly two values is listed**, not said as a range («los
  domingos y lunes»; «a las 9:00 y 10:00»). Outside the weekdays, two
  values that wrap are listed in ascending order: «a las 0:00 y 23:00»;
  «en enero y diciembre». Two hours can still be a window.
* **Pieces are said wherever the set has a run of three or more**: «de
  martes a jueves y los domingos»; «los lunes y de miércoles a viernes»
  **(reader 1** rewrote «los lunes, miércoles, jueves y viernes» this
  way**)**.
* **An exception** is taken from every value or from a cadence, never
  from a run; it must replace a list of more than seven values; and it
  names fewer things than the run or pieces would, counting one for an
  excepted value and two for an excepted window (3.9). It never removes a
  value the sentence has just named. Months take none; the days of the
  month take none in a union (4.3); years take one from a cadence or from
  every year (5.8).
* **Runs and cadences are said together** where that names fewer things
  than runs and listed values and replaces a list of more than seven:
  «cada 2 minutos del minuto 0 al 20 y cada 3 minutos del minuto 21 al
  57 de cada hora»; «en los años pares desde 2030 y en 2031» (3.10).
  Minutes, seconds, days of the month and years are said so; hours,
  months and weekdays never have enough values to need it.
* **A run with no end worth naming names one thing.** Years from the
  first year there is are «hasta 2030»; years with no end are «desde
  2030» (5.8).
* **Every range of times is closed: it names its first and its last
  moment, and both fire.** «De … a …» is read as including its end:
  «cada minuto de 9:00 a 17:59»; «cada 15 minutos de 9:00 a 17:45». There
  is no form in which a time that is named does not fire. **(Reader 1**
  understood every such window exactly, and noted that 17:59 "huele a
  máquina, pero es exacto"**.)**
* **There is no declared tie.** Hours 0 and 12 are «a las 0:00 y 12:00»
  however they were written. The first version said «cada 12 horas» for
  `*/12`; the reader could only guess when it fires.

## 1. Anchors

1.1 The Real Academia Española's *Ortografía* (2010) and the *Libro de
estilo de la lengua española* (2018): lower-case month and weekday names,
«y» with no comma before it in a list, a comma before «excepto», «a. m.»
and «p. m.» with a space; and the practice of timetables and interfaces in
Spain for the 24-hour clock with a colon.

1.2 The style table. Each field changes words or the form of one phrase,
never the order of the sentence.

| Field | Kind | Default | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hour12` | boolean | `false` | `false`: 24-hour times («a las 17:30»). `true`: 12-hour times with «a. m.» or «p. m.» after each: «a las 5:30 p. m.» |
| `short` | boolean | `false` | Weekday and month names in their three-letter forms («lun», «jun»), with no full stop; nothing else changes |

With `hour12: true` the hour is 12, 1 … 11 and every time carries its own
«a. m.» or «p. m.»; it is never shared: «de 9:00 a. m. a 5:45 p. m.». Hour
0 is «12», so 0:00 is «a las 12:00 a. m.» and 12:30 is «a las 12:30
p. m.». The article follows the hour as it is written: «a la 1:00 p. m.».

With `short: true` the names are «lun, mar, mié, jue, vie, sáb, dom» and
«ene, feb, mar, abr, may, jun, jul, ago, sep, oct, nov, dic»: «los lun,
mié y vie», «de lun a vie», «en jun», «los lun de jun», «el 1 de jun»,
«de jun a ago». «Los fines de semana» is not a name and does not change.
These are not the abbreviations of the *Ortografía*, which take a full
stop («lun.», «jun.»); they are the unpunctuated forms of calendar
headers and narrow columns, and the authority for this one style is that
practice **(schedule reader:** "not a sentence anyone writes"**)**. The
style is for a caller short of room; with full stops a list would read
«los lun., mié. y vie.». A fluent reviewer may prefer the stops.

With `hour12: true`, 0:00 and 12:00 are «12:00 a. m.» and «12:00 p. m.».
The *Diccionario panhispánico de dudas* advises against both as doubtful
**(schedule reader)**. They are kept because the alternatives mislead
more beside a date («a medianoche el día 1», «a las 12 de la noche del
día 1» are read by many as the night that ends the 1st), and because this
is what a phone or a computer in Mexico or the United States shows at
those two moments. Unresolved until a fluent reader from there has seen
it.

1.3 Regions. There are two named styles: `es`, the default, and `mx`,
which sets `hour12: true`. (Until the second repair there was a third
field, `workday`, choosing between «día laborable» and «día hábil»;
neither word is said any more, 5.7, and the field is gone.) Spain (`ES`) selects
`es`; Mexico (`MX`) and the United States (`US`) select `mx`. Any other
region takes the default.

## 2. Frame

2.1 One order, from the calendar to the clock: **a leading frame (the
years and months, when they stand apart), then the day, then the time**.
When a day of the month and a weekday are both restricted, the days of
the month come after the time, as an addition (4.3); that is the one
thing that ever follows the time.

* `0 9 * * *` — «todos los días a las 9:00»
* `*/15 9-17 * * 1-5` — «de lunes a viernes, cada 15 minutos de 9:00 a
  17:45»
* `0 0 1 * *` — «el día 1 de cada mes a las 0:00»
* `0 9 * 6-8 *` — «de junio a agosto, todos los días a las 9:00»
* `0 0 9 * * * 2030` — «en 2030, todos los días a las 9:00»
* `* * * * *` — «cada minuto»

The joins. There are three, and each has one rule.

* **After the leading frame: always a comma**, after each of its phrases:
  «en 2030 y 2031, en junio, todos los días a las 9:00».
* **Between the day and the time: a space before a clock time, a comma
  before anything else.** A time part that begins «a las» or «a la»
  follows the day with a space: «los lunes a las 9:00»; «los lunes,
  miércoles y viernes a las 9:00»; «los días 1, 10 y 20 de cada mes a las
  9:00»; «el día 1 de marzo, junio, septiembre y diciembre a las 0:00»;
  «todos los días a las 5:00 y cada hora de 9:00 a 17:00». Any other time
  part follows a comma: «los lunes, cada hora en punto»; «de lunes a viernes, cada
  15 minutos de 9:00 a 17:45»; «el día 1 de cada mes, cada minuto». The
  commas inside a list in the day part have nothing to do with it, and
  neither has any option.
* **An inserted phrase is closed by a comma when anything follows it.**
  The inserted phrase is an exception («, excepto …»). So a clock time
  after one follows a comma after all: «todos los días, excepto los
  miércoles, a las 9:00».
* **A day counted from the last day of the month is closed by a comma
  too** (5.7): «3 días antes del último día de cada mes, a las 0:00».
  Without it the time is heard with «el último día». **So is a day moved
  off the weekend** (5.7): «el día 15 de cada mes o, si cae en sábado o
  domingo, el viernes o el lunes más cercano, a las 0:00». Without it the
  time is heard with the Friday or the Monday only.
* **The addition of a union follows a comma**, after everything else:
  «los viernes a las 0:00, y además el día 1 de cada mes». Where the
  addition has to say more than its days (the leap years, the months long
  enough, or months of its own: 4.3, 5.7), that phrase stands between
  commas after «y además»: «los viernes a las 0:00, y además, en los
  meses de más de 30 días, 30 días antes del último día del mes».
* **Pieces are joined as a list is; a piece before the last that has an
  «y» of its own is closed by a comma** before the «y» that joins the
  last piece: «en enero y febrero, y de junio a agosto»; «a las 0:00 y
  1:00, y cada hora de 5:00 a 9:00»; «los días 1 y 2, y del 29 al 31 de
  cada mes». Without it the reader takes the second «y» for one more item
  of the first piece. A last piece may have its own «y» freely: «cada
  hora de 0:00 a 2:00 y a las 22:00 y 23:00».
* With no day part, the leading frame is followed by the time: «en junio,
  cada 5 minutos».

**(Reader 2** and the **schedule reader** both found the second version
putting a comma before «a las» after some lists and not others; it made
the comma depend on whether the day part held a comma of its own.**)**

2.2 Each coarser part is added in front of the parts finer than it.
Nothing moves, apart from the month and the single year that a date takes
into itself (4.4, 4.5).

* `0 9 * * 1` — «los lunes a las 9:00»
* `0 9 * 6 1` — «los lunes de junio a las 9:00»
* `0 9 * 6 1-5` — «en junio, de lunes a viernes a las 9:00»
* `0 9 * 6 *` — «en junio, todos los días a las 9:00»

2.3 Seconds come last, after the time they divide (4.2): «todos los días
a las 9:00:30»; «todos los días
a las 9:00, una vez por segundo durante todo ese minuto».

2.4 Why this order. The first version said the time first («a las 9:00
todos los días»). It was changed for three reasons.

* It is the spontaneous order **(reader 1:** «todos los días a las 9:00»,
  «el día 1 de cada mes a las 0:00» and «los lunes de junio a las 9:00»
  were each ranked first; "un hablante pone primero el marco"**)**.
* It removes a wrong reading. «Cada 2 horas, excepto a las 8:00, el día 1
  de cada mes» was read by the reader as possibly "every 2 hours always,
  except at 8:00 on the 1st". With the time last, an exception to the
  time ends the sentence and has nothing after it to attach to: «el día 1
  de cada mes, cada 2 horas de 0:00 a 22:00, excepto a las 8:00».
* A month or a year said first plainly covers everything after it. «A
  las 0:00 los días 1 y 15, y también de lunes a viernes, en enero y
  julio» had to be re-read to see what «en enero y julio» governs.

Its cost fell on the union of a day of the month and a weekday: with
both in front of the time, three readers in a row could not tell what
the time covered or whether the second side was a condition. 4.3 has the
cure, which puts the time after the first side.

Rejected: time first (above); and a strict order of year, month, day,
time with nothing fused («en junio, el día 1 a las 0:00»), because a date
is one phrase in Spanish, «el 1 de junio».

2.5 «Todos los días». A time of day needs a day to stand on. When the
hours are restricted and neither the day of the month nor the weekday is,
the day part says «todos los días»:

* `0 9 * * *` — «todos los días a las 9:00»
* `0 9-17 * * *` — «todos los días, cada hora de 9:00 a 17:00»
* `0 */2 * * *` — «todos los días, cada 2 horas de 0:00 a 22:00»
* `* 9,17 * * *` — «todos los días, cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:59 y de 17:00
  a 17:59»
* `0 9 * 6 *` — «en junio, todos los días a las 9:00»

It is not said when the hours are every hour, with or without exceptions:
«cada hora en punto»; «cada 15 minutos»; «en el minuto 30 de cada hora»;
«cada hora en punto, excepto a las 3:00»; «en junio, cada 5 minutos». A named day of
either kind, or a Quartz form, replaces it. An edit to the hours may add
or remove «todos los días», and with it the join; no other field's words
depend on the time.

2.6 Punctuation that belongs to a join and not to a part: the three
joins of 2.1.

2.7 What one part takes from another, all declared here and in
`continuity.js`:

* the month, and a single year after it, join a date or a weekday phrase
  in place of leading (4.4, 4.5);
* a day list or range under named months loses the days those months do
  not have, and where a named month has none of the days, only the months
  that fire are said (4.4);
* a schedule whose days exist in no month it reaches says «nunca» and no
  time (4.4): the one place where the day, the month or the year changes
  the time part, by removing it;
* in a union the days of the month leave the day part and follow the
  time, take no exception there, and under named months say «de ese mes»
  or «de esos meses» in place of «de cada mes», or the months of their
  own that they fire in (4.3);
* a set of weekdays missing one day is an exception alone and a run
  beside a day of the month (3.9);
* days of the month that are every day of the months named leave the
  weekdays nothing to add, and the sentence says «todos los días» (4.3);
* a 29th of February with no other month leads with «en los años
  bisiestos», and a day counted from the last that a month may be too
  short for leads with the months that fire (4.4, 5.7);
* hours that run past midnight are said as one run only when no day,
  month or year is restricted (3.5);
* «todos los días» (2.5).

## 3. Devices, field by field

Numbers are digits everywhere. The only number words are the ordinals of
a Quartz «segundo lunes» (5.7).

3.1 Every value: silent, except that a time with nothing else to say is
«cada minuto», and with S every second, «cada segundo».

* `* * * * *` — «cada minuto»
* `0 * * * *` — «cada hora en punto» (5.4)

3.2 One value.

* Minute, with H every hour: «en el minuto 30 de cada hora». Spanish
  numbers the minutes of an hour from 0, so «el minuto 0» and «el minuto
  1» mean :00 and :01 with no doubt. **(Reader 1:** "clara pero suena a
  técnico"; against «a y media de cada hora», «cada hora a los 30
  minutos» and «a las y media de cada hora» it was ranked first, "la
  única que nadie entiende mal".**)**
* Minute with one hour: a clock time, «a las 9:30» (4.1).
* Hour with minute 0: «a las 9:00».
* Day of the month: «el día 15 de cada mes».
* Month: «en junio», leading; or inside a date, «el 15 de junio».
* Weekday: «los lunes».

3.3 A list. Items are joined with commas and a final «y», with no comma
before it. (No word that can follow «y» here begins with the sound /i/,
so it never becomes «e».)

* Minutes: «en los minutos 15 y 45 de cada hora».
* Hours with minute 0: «a las 8:00, 12:00 y 18:00». The preposition and
  article are said once, unless the list begins at hour 1 and goes on to
  another hour (4.1).
* Days of the month: «los días 1, 10 y 20 de cada mes».
* Months: «en enero y julio».
* Weekdays: «los lunes, miércoles y viernes». One article for the list.
  The list is in week order starting on Monday, with Sunday last. The one
  exception is an unbroken run crossing from Sunday into Monday, said in
  the order of the run: «los domingos y lunes»; «de sábado a lunes».
  Sunday and Monday alone keep that order only when they are the whole
  set; beside other days they are listed in week order, «los lunes,
  miércoles, viernes y domingos». A run of three days or more across
  Sunday is a run wherever it stands, and leads its pieces (3.6): «de
  domingo a martes y los jueves».

3.4 A range is «de … a …», and both ends fire.

* Hours as times on the hour: «cada hora de 9:00 a 17:00».
* Days: «del 1 al 7 de cada mes». Months: «de junio a agosto». Years: «de
  2030 a 2035». Weekdays: «de martes a jueves», with no article and no
  plural.
* A stretch of time filled by a finer cadence is a window that names its
  last moment: hours 9 to 17 under every minute are «cada minuto de 9:00 a
  17:59»; under a cadence the window closes on the last run, «cada 15
  minutos de 9:00 a 17:45».
* A run of minutes inside one named hour: «cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:29».
* A run of minutes under every hour or a stretch of hours: one that starts
  at 0 is «durante los primeros 30 minutos»: «cada minuto durante los
  primeros 30 minutos de cada hora». One that ends at 59 is «durante los
  últimos 15 minutos». Any other is «del minuto 10 al 40»: «cada minuto
  del minuto 10 al 40 de cada hora». Seconds the same: «cada segundo
  durante los primeros 10 segundos de cada minuto»; «del segundo 10 al
  40».

3.5 A range that wraps is said from its start to its end, the same way:
«cada hora de 22:00 a 2:00», «de noviembre a febrero», «de viernes a
lunes». As a window: «cada minuto de 22:00 a 2:59».

A wrap is only true when nothing coarser is restricted:

* hours wrap only when the day of the month, the weekday, the month and
  the year are all every value;
* **days of the month never wrap.** They are said in ascending order:
  `0 0 28-3 * *` is «del 1 al 3 y del 28 al 31 de cada mes a las 0:00»;
  `0 0 29-2 * *`, «los días 1 y 2, y del 29 al 31 de cada mes a las
  0:00». Until the second repair such a run was said across the months,
  «del 28 de cada mes al 3 del siguiente». That is only true of a run
  that starts on a day every month has: «del 29 de cada mes al 2 del
  siguiente» promises nothing after a February of 28 days, where the 1st
  and 2nd of March fire all the same; and over most of the month («del 20
  de cada mes al 10 del siguiente») it reads as a mistake. The ascending
  form names four things in place of two, needs no working out, and is
  what the same days are said as under a named month. Rejected: keeping
  the wrap for runs that start by the 28th, which would make the form
  depend on which days are named;
* months wrap only when the year is;
* weekdays may always wrap;
* minutes and seconds follow 3.6.

Where a wrap is not allowed, the set is described in ascending order:
`0 22-2 1 * *` is «el día 1 de cada mes, cada hora de 0:00 a 2:00 y a las
22:00 y 23:00». The hours of a named day are that day's hours: its first
three and its last two, not a night that begins on it.

A wrap of exactly two values is not a run at all: hours 23 and 0 are «a
las 0:00 y 23:00», months 12 and 1 «en enero y diciembre». Only weekdays
keep the order of the run, «los domingos y lunes».

3.6 Pieces are joined as a list is, each with its own lead: «cada hora de
9:00 a 17:00 y a las 22:00»; «cada minuto de 9:00 a 17:59 y de 22:00 a
22:59»; «del 1 al 7 y el día 15 de cada mes»; «de junio a agosto y en
diciembre»; «de martes a viernes y los domingos»; three or more, «del 1 al
5, el día 10 y del 20 al 25 de cada mes».

The lead of one piece never reaches over another. Among hours on the
hour, a run is led by «cada hora» and a stray hour by «a las», whichever
comes first: `0 5,9-17 * * *` is «todos los días a las 5:00 y cada hora
de 9:00 a 17:00», never «cada hora a las 5:00 y de 9:00 a 17:00».

Pieces are said in ascending order of their first value, unless one of
the runs wraps round the cycle, in which case the wrapped run comes first:
`0 20-4,9-12 * * *` is «todos los días, cada hora de 20:00 a 4:00 y de
9:00 a 12:00». (Ascending order would split the night in two, «de 0:00 a
4:00, de 9:00 a 12:00 y de 20:00 a 23:00», three pieces for two.) Weekday
pieces follow the week order of 3.3, «los lunes y de miércoles a
viernes», and a run across Sunday leads them in the same way.

A run of minutes with a stray minute: «cada minuto durante los primeros
30 minutos y en el minuto 45 de cada hora»; «cada minuto del minuto 5 al
10 y en el minuto 20 de cada hora». A run of minutes that wraps inside
one hour is two windows: «cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:10 y de 9:50 a 9:59».

3.7 A cadence, field by field.

* **Minutes** are bare when the cadence tiles the hour (the interval
  divides 60 and it starts at 0): «cada 15 minutos»; minutes 0 and 30 are
  «cada 30 minutos». Every speaker counts minutes from the hour, and both
  readers took «cada 15 minutos de 9:00 a 17:45» exactly. A cadence that
  does not tile, starts off the hour, or starts on it and stops early is
  listed up to seven minutes and from eight names both ends: `5/10` is «en
  los minutos 5, 15, 25, 35, 45 y 55 de cada hora»; `0-40/10` «en los
  minutos 0, 10, 20, 30 y 40 de cada hora»; `*/7` «cada 7 minutos del
  minuto 0 al 56 de cada hora»; `0-42/6` «cada 6 minutos del minuto 0 al
  42 de cada hora». A start at 0 does not make a cadence bare: only
  tiling does. Every second minute from 1 is «en los minutos impares de
  cada hora». Inside a named hour a cadence is a window of clock times
  (4.1).
* **Seconds** the same, on the minute: «cada 15 segundos»; «en los
  segundos 5, 15, 25, 35, 45 y 55 de cada minuto»; «en los segundos
  impares de cada minuto».
* **Hours are never bare.** Up to seven hours are listed as times: `*/6`
  is «a las 0:00, 6:00, 12:00 y 18:00»; `*/12` «a las 0:00 y 12:00». From
  eight the cadence names both ends as clock times, and carries the
  minute: `*/2` is «cada 2 horas de 0:00 a 22:00»; `1/2` «cada 2 horas de
  1:00 a 23:00»; `0-20/2` «cada 2 horas de 0:00 a 20:00»; `30 */3` «cada 3
  horas de 0:30 a 21:30». **(Both readers** took these exactly.**)**
  Rejected: «cada 2 horas desde las 0:00», which names the start and
  leaves the reader to find the last; «las horas pares».
* **Days of the month** never tile. Every second day is an idiom (5.1),
  and one that starts late or stops early keeps it between its ends, from
  eight days: `8/2` is «los días pares del 8 al 30 de cada mes». Any
  other cadence is listed up to seven days, `*/5` «los días 1, 6, 11, 16,
  21, 26 y 31 de cada mes», and from eight names both ends: `*/3` is
  «cada 3 días del 1 al 31 de cada mes».
* **Months** have no cadence and no idiom. The months are named, every
  second month too: «en enero, marzo, mayo, julio, septiembre y
  noviembre». **(Reader 1** on «los meses impares»: "nadie piensa en meses
  impares; hay que numerar los meses".**)**
* **Weekdays** have no cadence. The days are listed.
* **Years**: 5.8.

3.8 Where the hours of a cadence are filled by minutes (M is every minute
or a bare cadence), they are not points and take the forms of 4.1:
windows for up to four hours, and beyond four the rule with its bracket.

3.9 A set with values missing is said as a simpler set «excepto» the
missing ones. A comma comes before «excepto», and another closes the
exception when anything follows it: «los días impares de cada mes,
excepto el día 15, a las 9:00»; «todos los días, excepto los miércoles, a
las 9:00». At the end of the sentence there is no closing comma: «cada
hora en punto, excepto a las 3:00». **(Reader 1:** the comma before
«excepto» is needed; «salvo» is as good and a little more formal; «menos» is misread
at first as "cada 2 horas menos".**)**

**What it is taken from.** Every value, or a cadence (an idiom such as
«los días impares» is a cadence). Never a run: the core also offers a run
with holes in it, and it is not said. `* 9-17,20,22 * * *` is three
windows, «todos los días, cada minuto de 9:00 a 17:59, de 20:00 a 20:59 y
de 22:00 a 22:59».

**When.** When the set has more than seven values and the exception
names fewer things than the run, the pieces or the cadence with its ends
would. An excepted value counts one; an excepted hour that is filled by
minutes is a window, which names two times, and counts two; a run counts
two and a listed value one; every value counts nothing and a cadence two,
for its ends. A set that is itself a cadence is said as the cadence, and
no exception is weighed against it. On a tie the run or the pieces are
said, with one exception: against a run that wraps, a tie goes to the
exception, because a reader has to work out what a wrapped run leaves
out, and the exception names it. So `0 0-2,5-23 * * *` is «cada hora en
punto, excepto a las 3:00 y 4:00» (two things against the two ends of «de 5:00 a
2:00»), and `* 0-2,5-23 * * *`, where the two excepted hours are windows
and count four, is the run, «todos los días, cada minuto de 5:00 a 2:59».
Beside a named day the hours do not wrap (3.5), and the same sets are
weighed against their ascending pieces: `* 0-2,4-23 * * 1` is «los lunes,
cada minuto, excepto de 3:00 a 3:59» (two against four), and `* 0-2,5-23
* * 1` is «los lunes, cada minuto de 0:00 a 2:59 y de 5:00 a 23:59» (four
against four). Days of the month the same, against their ascending
pieces: `0 0 8-6 * *` is «todos los días de cada mes, excepto el día 7, a
las 0:00» (one against four), and `0 0 6-2 * *`, three days short, is
«todos los días de cada mes, excepto los días 3, 4 y 5, a las 0:00»
(three against four).

* Taken from every value, the hole may be anywhere, at an end too. A set
  of points one value short of everything is one thing as an exception
  and two as a run: `0 0-22 * * *` is «cada hora en punto, excepto a las
  23:00»; `0-58 * * * *` «cada minuto, excepto en el minuto 59 de cada
  hora»; `0 0 2-31 * *` «todos los días de cada mes, excepto el día 1, a
  las 0:00». Two values short at one end tie with the run, and a run that
  does not wrap wins a tie: `0 0-21 * * *` is «todos los días, cada hora
  de 0:00 a 21:00». Filled hours one short at an end tie as well, and are
  the run: `* 0-22 * * *` is «todos los días, cada minuto de 0:00 a
  22:59». A hole in the middle ties with a run that wraps, and there the
  exception is said: «cada 10 minutos, excepto de 3:00 a 3:50».
* **But a window never names a moment that an exception then removes.**
  A minute missing at the very start or end of the hour is an exception
  only under every hour. Inside named hours the minutes are a window of
  clock times, and the missing minute moves the end of the window:
  `1-59 9 * * *` is «todos los días, cada minuto de 9:01 a 9:59», never
  «de 9:00 a 9:59, excepto a las 9:00»; `0-58 9-17 * * *` is «todos los
  días, cada minuto durante los primeros 59 minutos de cada hora, de
  9:00 a 17:58». A second missing at the edge of the minute is an
  exception only under every minute of every hour («cada segundo,
  excepto en el segundo 0 de cada minuto»); otherwise it is a run:
  `1-59 0 9 * * *` is «todos los días a las 9:00, una vez por segundo
  durante los últimos 59 segundos».
* Taken from a cadence, the hole must be in the middle. A cadence that is
  only missing values at its end or its start is said with its bounds:
  «cada 2 horas de 0:00 a 20:00». And the hole must come after every
  value the cadence shows: the first two stretches of the bracket of 4.1,
  the first three years of a series (5.8). `* 0,4-22/2 * * *` would
  otherwise show «de 2:00 a 2:59» in its bracket and then except it; it
  is said by its hours instead (4.1).
* Where an exception is not said and the set is one cadence with holes,
  the cadence is said in its stretches (3.10).

**The forms.**

* Hours as points: «cada hora en punto, excepto a las 3:00»; «en el minuto 30 de
  cada hora, excepto a las 3:30»; from a cadence, «cada 2 horas de 0:00 a
  22:00, excepto a las 8:00». The cadence names its ends, so the reader
  can see that 8:00 was in it **(reader 1:** with a bare «cada 2 horas» "el
  lector no sabe si las 8:00 estaban en ella"**)**.
* Hours that are filled: a window from the first time that would have
  fired in that hour to the last. «Cada 10 minutos, excepto de 3:00 a
  3:50»; «cada minuto, excepto de 3:00 a 3:59»; «en los minutos 5, 20 y 45
  de cada hora, excepto de 3:05 a 3:45»; after the bracket of 4.1, «…
  hasta las 22:45), excepto de 8:00 a 8:45».
* Minutes: said last in the time part. Under every hour or a stretch of
  hours, «cada minuto, excepto en el minuto 29 de cada hora»; «cada 10
  minutos de 9:00 a 17:50, excepto en el minuto 30 de cada hora». Inside
  one named hour, or up to four, it is clock times: «cada 10 minutos de
  9:00 a 9:50, excepto a las 9:30»; «cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:59 y de 17:00
  a 17:59, excepto a las 9:29 y 17:29». That holds while the excepted
  times name four things at most. Beyond four the hours would be
  multiplied by the minutes (three minutes in four hours are twelve
  times), and the exception is said once, of each hour: `0-24,28-59 9,17
  * * *` is «todos los días, cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:59 y de 17:00 a
  17:59, excepto en los minutos 25, 26 y 27 de cada hora». After windows
  that name their hours, «de cada hora» is read as each of those hours.
  If the hours have an exception
  too, «excepto» is said once and the hours' comes first: «cada 10
  minutos, excepto de 3:00 a 3:50 y en el minuto 30 de cada hora».
* Seconds: «cada segundo, excepto en el segundo 29 de cada minuto»; after
  «durante todo ese minuto», «excepto en el segundo 29»: «a las 9:00, una
  vez por segundo durante todo ese minuto, excepto en el segundo 29». Under «cada segundo»
  an excepted minute is a whole minute: «cada segundo, excepto durante el
  minuto 29 de cada hora»; inside one hour, or up to four, a window to
  the second, «excepto de 9:29:00 a 9:29:59». Excepted minutes that
  follow one another are one window, «excepto de 9:25:00 a 9:27:59». A
  window counts two, so beyond two windows the exception is said of each
  hour: `* 0-28,30-59 */6 * * *` ends «…, excepto durante el minuto 29 de
  cada hora».
* Days of the month: «los días impares de cada mes, excepto el día 15»;
  every day but a few, «todos los días de cada mes, excepto el día 15»;
  «todos los días de cada mes, excepto los días 15 y 16»; in one month,
  «todos los días de junio, excepto el día 15». **(Reader 1:** "mejor aún
  «excepto el día 15»".**)**
* Weekdays: six days are «todos los días, excepto» and the seventh,
  «todos los días, excepto los miércoles». This is the only exception a
  set of weekdays takes, and it is exempt from the count of seven.
  Monday to Saturday and Tuesday to Sunday are not said this way: neither
  wraps the week, which starts on Monday, and each is always the run, «de
  lunes a sábado», «de martes a domingo». In a union the exception is not
  said, so that the sentence has one «excepto» at most outside the time;
  there the six days are the run that wraps: «de jueves a martes a las
  0:00, y además el día 15 de cada mes».
* **Months take no exception.** They lead the sentence, where an
  exception has nothing to be taken from; all months but December are «de
  enero a noviembre», and months with a hole are a run that wraps where
  the months may wrap (3.5), «de julio a mayo», and pieces where they may
  not: «de enero a noviembre, todos los días a las 9:00».
* Years: 5.8.

3.10 Runs and cadences together. Some sets are no single run, cadence or
short list: minutes 0 to 20 by twos and then 21 to 57 by threes; the odd
days with the 15th missing, in a union, where no exception is said; every
second year from 2030, and 2031. The core offers such a set as pieces
that may be cadences (`cadencePieces`), and a cadence with holes can
also be cut at its holes. A set is said this way when it has more than
seven values and the pieces name fewer things than its runs and listed
values would; an exception that names no more things is still preferred,
because it names one set.

* A piece that is a cadence is said as the field says a cadence between
  two ends (3.7): «cada 2 minutos del minuto 0 al 20»; «los días impares
  del 1 al 15»; «cada 3 días del 1 al 28»; inside a named hour, «cada 2
  minutos de 9:00 a 9:20». A cadence the field would list (seven values
  or fewer, 3.7) is listed here too, with any loose values: `0 0
  1-15/2,16-31/3 * *` is «los días impares del 1 al 15 y los días 16, 19,
  22, 25, 28 y 31 de cada mes a las 0:00».
* The stretches of one cadence cut at its holes share its lead, and each
  stretch of three values or more keeps its ends: «los días impares del 1
  al 13 y del 17 al 31 de cada mes». Where the core's pieces and the
  stretches of one cadence name as many things, the stretches are said:
  `2028,2036/4` is «en 2028 y en 2036, 2040, 2044 y así sucesivamente»,
  not two series of every eighth year.
* A cadence that fills the cycle still names its ends beside another
  piece: `*/5,7 * * * *` is «cada 5 minutos del minuto 0 al 55 y en el
  minuto 7 de cada hora».
* **Inside a cycle the runs and cadences must follow one another.** Two
  cadences that run through each other («cada 4 días del 1 al 29 y …
  del 3 al 31») cannot be held, and such a set is listed. A loose value
  may fall anywhere. Years have no cycle, and a series may run through
  another: «en 2030, 2034, 2038 y así sucesivamente, y en 2031, 2035,
  2039 y así sucesivamente».
* The pieces are said in ascending order of their first value. Loose
  values that stand next to each other in that order are one list, in
  ascending order.
* Counted as things named: two for a run or a cadence between two ends,
  one for each listed value, one for a run or a series with an end not
  worth naming (5.8).
* Hours, months and weekdays are never said this way: the longest list
  they can need is twelve hours.

`0-20/2,21-59/3 * * * *` — «cada 2 minutos del minuto 0 al 20 y cada 3
minutos del minuto 21 al 57 de cada hora». `0-20/2,21-59/3 9 * * *` —
«todos los días, cada 2 minutos de 9:00 a 9:20 y cada 3 minutos de 9:21 a
9:57». Before this rule the first was a list of twenty-four minutes.

## 4. Fusions

4.1 Clock times. The time part is built from M and H together, by these
cases in order. It is built as if S were 0; 4.2 adds the seconds.

**M is a single minute.** The hours are points.

| H | Minute 0 | Minute 30 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| every hour | «cada hora en punto» | «en el minuto 30 de cada hora» |
| values | «a las 9:00 y 17:00» | «a las 9:30 y 17:30» |
| range | «cada hora de 9:00 a 17:00» | «cada hora de 9:30 a 17:30» |
| pieces | «cada hora de 9:00 a 17:00 y a las 22:00» | «cada hora de 9:30 a 17:30 y a las 22:30» |
| a cadence of seven hours or fewer | «a las 0:00, 6:00, 12:00 y 18:00» | «a las 0:30, 6:30, 12:30 y 18:30» |
| a cadence of eight hours or more | «cada 2 horas de 0:00 a 22:00» | «cada 2 horas de 0:30 a 22:30» |
| a cadence with exceptions | «cada 2 horas de 0:00 a 22:00, excepto a las 8:00» | «cada 2 horas de 0:30 a 22:30, excepto a las 8:30» |
| every hour with exceptions | «cada hora en punto, excepto a las 3:00» | «en el minuto 30 de cada hora, excepto a las 3:30» |

**M is continuous** (every minute, or a bare cadence). The lead is «cada
minuto» or «cada N minutos», followed by H as a confinement. A run of
hours, and an hour standing alone, is a window.

| H | Confinement |
| --- | --- |
| every hour | none |
| one hour | «cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:59»; «cada 15 minutos de 9:00 a 9:45» |
| a range | «cada minuto de 9:00 a 17:59»; «cada 15 minutos de 9:00 a 17:45» |
| pieces, at least one of them a run | each a window: «cada 15 minutos de 9:00 a 17:45 y de 22:00 a 22:45»; «cada minuto de 9:00 a 17:59, de 20:00 a 20:59 y de 22:00 a 22:59» |
| separate hours, four or fewer | each a window: «cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:59 y de 17:00 a 17:59»; «cada 15 minutos de 8:00 a 8:45, de 12:00 a 12:45 y de 18:00 a 18:45»; hours 0, 6, 12 and 18, «cada minuto de 0:00 a 0:59, de 6:00 a 6:59, de 12:00 a 12:59 y de 18:00 a 18:59» |
| a cadence of more than four hours | the rule, then the stretches in brackets: «durante una hora cada N horas», and in brackets the first two windows, «…», and «hasta las» with the last moment that fires. `* */2` is «cada minuto durante una hora cada 2 horas (de 0:00 a 0:59, de 2:00 a 2:59… hasta las 22:59)»; `*/15 1/2`, «cada 15 minutos durante una hora cada 2 horas (de 1:00 a 1:45, de 3:00 a 3:45… hasta las 23:45)»; `* */3`, «cada minuto durante una hora cada 3 horas (de 0:00 a 0:59, de 3:00 a 3:59… hasta las 21:59)»; `*/15 8-18/2`, «cada 15 minutos durante una hora cada 2 horas (de 8:00 a 8:45, de 10:00 a 10:45… hasta las 18:45)» |
| a cadence of more than four hours, with exceptions | the same, then the exception after the bracket: «… hasta las 22:45), excepto de 8:00 a 8:45» |
| more than four separate hours that form no cadence | the hours by when they begin: «cada minuto durante las horas que empiezan a las 6:00, 8:00, 12:00, 18:00 y 20:00» |
| every hour with exceptions | «cada 10 minutos, excepto de 3:00 a 3:50» |

This is the fourth attempt at every second hour, and each earlier one
failed on a reader. «Las horas pares»: "el lector duda de si las 0
cuentan" (reader 1). «En horas alternas desde las 0:00»: "no dice cuánto
dura cada tramo" (reader 2). Three windows and «y así sucesivamente hasta
las 22:59»: reader 3 had to re-read, "el lector debe deducir la serie y
comprobar que «hasta las 22:59» significa «de 22:00 a 22:59»". Readers 2
and 3 each wrote the same cure unprompted: name the rule, then show the
stretches in brackets, «(de 0:00 a 0:59, de 2:00 a 2:59… hasta las
22:59)». Their rule was «durante las horas pares», which exists only for
every second hour from 0; «durante una hora cada N horas» says the same
for any interval and any start, and the bracket settles which hours and
how long. «Las horas que empiezan a las» remains only for five or more
hours with no cadence; it is clumsy and cannot be misread. An hour is
never named by a bare number («las horas 8, 12 y 18» "no es español",
reader 1).

**M is a single run of minutes, or a cadence that is not bare, and H is
one hour or up to four separate hours.** A window of clock times in each
hour: «cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:29»; «de 9:10 a 9:40»; «cada 7 minutos de
9:00 a 9:56»; `5/15 9` «cada 15 minutos de 9:05 a 9:50»; with two hours,
«cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:29 y de 17:00 a 17:29»; `5/10 9,17 * * *` is
«todos los días, cada 10 minutos de 9:05 a 9:55 y de 17:05 a 17:55»; with
three, «cada minuto de 8:00 a 8:29, de 12:00 a 12:29 y de 18:00 a 18:29».
A cadence of three minutes or fewer is listed as times instead («a las
9:00, 9:20 y 9:40»). A run with a stray minute in one hour adds it: «cada
minuto de 9:00 a 9:29 y a las 9:45»; so do several runs in one hour, each
a window («cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:10 y de 9:50 a 9:59»).

**M is a single run of minutes and H is a cadence of more than four
hours.** The bracket of the table above, with the length of the run in
place of «una hora»: `0-29 */2 * * *` is «todos los días, cada minuto
durante 30 minutos cada 2 horas (de 0:00 a 0:29, de 2:00 a 2:29… hasta
las 22:29)»; `10-40 */2`, «cada minuto durante 31 minutos cada 2 horas
(de 0:10 a 0:40, de 2:10 a 2:40… hasta las 22:40)». Until the second
repair this named twelve hours by when they begin.

That is for one hour only. Minutes in several parts (a run with a stray
minute, several runs) inside two to four separate hours are not written
out hour by hour: every part would be said again for every hour («cada
minuto de 9:00 a 9:29, a las 9:45, cada minuto de 17:00 a 17:29 y a las
17:45», and with four hours eight pieces). They are anchored, as below,
and the hours are named by when they begin: `0-29,45 9,17 * * *` is
«todos los días, cada minuto durante los primeros 30 minutos y en el
minuto 45 de las horas que empiezan a las 9:00 y 17:00». Counted as
things named, the parts and the hours are added in this form and
multiplied in the other.

**Otherwise M is anchored**: a list, a run, pieces, an exception, or a
cadence that is not bare. The minute phrase from section 3 is followed by
H:

| H | After the minute phrase |
| --- | --- |
| every hour | «de cada hora» |
| a range, of two hours or more | «de cada hora», a comma, and a window from the first time that fires to the last: «en los minutos 5, 20 y 45 de cada hora, de 9:05 a 17:45»; «cada minuto durante los primeros 30 minutos de cada hora, de 9:00 a 17:29»; «en los minutos 5, 20 y 45 de cada hora, de 9:05 a 10:45». (A window of hours, «de 9:00 a 17:00», would be read as stopping before the last hour's minutes.) |
| pieces | windows the same way: «de cada hora, de 9:05 a 17:45 y de 22:05 a 22:45» |
| separate hours, four or fewer, or with no cadence | no minute phrase: the clock times are listed, while there are at most twelve («a las 9:05, 9:20, 9:45, 17:05, 17:20 y 17:45»; `15,45 */6`, «a las 0:15, 0:45, 6:15, 6:45, 12:15, 12:45, 18:15 y 18:45»). Beyond twelve: «de las horas que empiezan a las 6:00, 8:00, 12:00, 18:00 y 20:00» |
| a cadence of more than four hours, at most twelve times | the clock times are listed like any others: `15,45 */4` is «a las 0:15, 0:45, 4:15, 4:45, 8:15, 8:45, 12:15, 12:45, 16:15, 16:45, 20:15 y 20:45». (The third version cut this to six times and «y así sucesivamente hasta las 20:45»; reader 3 "tuvo que hacer cuentas para sacar la regla". Reader 2 took the full list at first reading.) |
| a cadence of more than four hours, more than twelve times | one cadence of hours with a range of clock times for each minute, up to four minutes: `15,45 */2` is «cada 2 horas de 0:15 a 22:15 y de 0:45 a 22:45». It is the form of 3.7, which every reader has taken exactly. With more than four minutes, «de las horas que empiezan a las …» |
| every hour with exceptions | «de cada hora, excepto de 3:05 a 3:45» |

**Hours named by when they begin follow the rule for any cadence
(3.7):** up to seven are listed, «de las horas que empiezan a las 0:00,
4:00, 8:00, 12:00, 16:00 y 20:00», and a cadence of eight hours or more
names its ends, «de las horas que empiezan cada 2 horas de 0:00 a
22:00». `0-29,45 */2 * * *` is «todos los días, cada minuto durante los
primeros 30 minutos y en el minuto 45 de las horas que empiezan cada 2
horas de 0:00 a 22:00»; an excepted hour follows as a window, «…, excepto
de 8:00 a 8:45». Hours with no cadence are listed however many they are.

The comma before the window is the reader's: «en los minutos 5, 20 y 45
de cada hora de 9:05 a 17:45 de lunes a viernes» was "tres complementos
seguidos sin pausa".

**A list of clock times is also sayable** whenever neither M nor H is
every value and there are at most twelve times: «a las 9:05, 9:20 y
9:45». It names one thing per time and competes with the anchored form by
the count of things named (a value or a clock time one each, a range or a
window two). On a tie the list of times wins. `0,30 9,17 * * *` is «a las
9:00, 9:30, 17:00 y 17:30»; `5,20,45 9-10 * * *` is the anchored form,
five things against six times.

**Beside several seconds named after it, a list of clock times goes up
to seven.** Twelve times are taken at a first reading because each is a
firing that needs no working out. When the seconds clause names more
than one second (a list, «en los segundos 0, 15, 30 y 45», or «en los
segundos impares»), the list is no longer the list of firings: every
time fires once for each second, and the reader multiplies. The list is
then held to seven, as long as a list of plain values gets (see «How a
form is chosen»), and beyond seven the time part takes the form it
takes beyond twelve, so that minutes, hours and seconds are each said
once, apart. `*/15 */25 2/6 * * *` was
«a las 2:00, 2:25, 2:50, 8:00, 8:25, 8:50, 14:00, 14:25, 14:50, 20:00,
20:25 y 20:50, siempre en los segundos 0, 15, 30 y 45», sixteen things
standing for forty-eight firings; it is «en los segundos 0, 15, 30 y 45
de los minutos 0, 25 y 50 de las horas que empiezan a las 2:00, 8:00,
14:00 y 20:00» (4.2, rule 2, the seconds joined to the minutes).
`0,30 15,45 */6 * * *` is «en los segundos 0 y 30 de los minutos 15 y 45
de las horas que empiezan a las 0:00, 6:00, 12:00 y 18:00»; `0,30 15,45
*/4 * * *`, twelve times in a cadence of six hours, is «cada 4 horas de
0:15 a 20:15 y de 0:45 a 20:45, siempre en los segundos 0 y 30». Seven
times or fewer stay a list: `*/15 */25 2,8 * * *` is «a las 2:00, 2:25,
2:50, 8:00, 8:25 y 8:50, siempre en los segundos 0, 15, 30 y 45». One
second is a tail on each time and a stretch of seconds is one burst
after each, so neither multiplies the list, and twelve times still
hold: «…, siempre en el segundo 30»; «…, una vez por segundo durante
todo ese minuto».

Writing a time: hours without a leading zero, always with minutes, a
colon between: «9:00», «17:30», «0:05». With seconds, «9:00:30»; in a
list where any time has seconds, every time shows them («a las 9:00:00 y
9:00:30»). The preposition and article are «a las», and «a la» before
hour 1 («a la 1:00», «a la 1:30»); 13:00 is «a las 13:00». A range takes
no article («de 1:00 a 13:00»).

In a list the preposition and article take their form from the first
time and are said once: «a las 9:00 y 17:00»; «a las 0:00, 1:00, 22:00 y
23:00»; «a la 1:05 y 1:20». **(Reader 1:** repeating them before each time
is "correcta pero pesada"; "en una lista nadie echa de menos «a la
1:00»".**)** They are said again only when the list begins at hour 1 and
goes on to another hour, where «a la» cannot cover what follows; then
they are said before every time: «a la 1:30 y a las 13:30» (which the
reader called "bien cuidada"); «a la 1:00, a las 5:00 y a las 9:00».
(Saying them a second time only, «a la 1:00, a las 5:00 y 9:00», leaves
the list without a shape.) The hour is taken as it is written, so with
`hour12: true` 13:00 is hour 1: «a la 1:00 a. m. y 1:00 p. m.»; «a las
9:00 a. m. y 1:00 p. m.».

4.2 Seconds. The time part is built as if S were 0 (4.1), and the seconds
are added to it by the first of these that applies. The windows of the
time part stay as they are, to the minute, in every case but the first:
the first version wrote them to the second («de 9:00:00 a 17:45:59»), and
reader 1 called that the precision of a machine. Such a window names the
first and the last minute in which the schedule fires; the seconds
phrase says when inside each of those minutes (8.5).

1. **M is every minute, or every minute with exceptions.** The seconds
   lead in place of «cada minuto». Every second or a bare cadence: «cada
   segundo»; «cada 30 segundos»; with hours, the window is written to the
   second, because «cada segundo de 9:00 a 17:59» would leave the last
   minute in doubt: «cada segundo de 9:00:00 a 17:59:59». A run of
   minutes is taken the same way: «cada segundo durante los primeros 30
   minutos de cada hora»; «cada segundo de 9:00:00 a 9:29:59». Anchored
   seconds: «en el segundo 30 de cada minuto»; «en los segundos 5, 15,
   25, 35, 45 y 55 de cada minuto»; «cada segundo durante los primeros 10
   segundos de cada minuto»; with hours, after a comma, «en el segundo 30
   de cada minuto, de 9:00 a 17:59».
2. **The time part names minutes of the hour by number, or is «cada
   hora».** With any second said, minute 0 under every hour is named as a
   minute, because «cada hora, en el segundo 30» was read by reader 3 as
   possibly second 30 of every minute: `30 0 * * * *` is «en el segundo
   30 del minuto 0 de cada hora»; `* 0 * * * *`, «cada segundo durante el
   minuto 0 de cada hora»; `0-9 0 * * * *`, «cada segundo durante los
   primeros 10 segundos del minuto 0 de cada hora». The seconds join the
   minute phrase: «en el segundo 15 del minuto 30 de cada hora»;
   «en los segundos 0 y 30 de los minutos 5 y 20 de cada hora»; «cada
   segundo durante el minuto 30 de cada hora»; «cada segundo durante los
   minutos 30 y 42 de cada hora, de 3:30 a 10:42».
3. **The time part is clock times that can carry the second.** A list of
   clock times which with the seconds is still four times or fewer: «a
   las 9:00:30»; «a las 9:00:00 y 9:00:30»; «a las 0:00:15, 6:00:15,
   12:00:15 y 18:00:15». And a single second under hours said as points
   between two clock times: `30 0 9-17 * * *` is «cada hora de 9:00:30 a
   17:00:30»; `15 0 */2 * * *`, «cada 2 horas de 0:00:15 a 22:00:15»; `8
   5,30 */2 * * *`, «cada 2 horas de 0:05:08 a 22:05:08 y de 0:30:08 a
   22:30:08».
4. **Otherwise the seconds are a clause after the time part.**
   * Seconds that are named follow a comma: «en el segundo 30»; «en los
     segundos 0 y 30»; «en los segundos 5, 15, 25, 35, 45 y 55»; every
     second second from 1, «en los segundos impares». `0,30 */15 9-17 *
     * 1-5` is «de lunes a viernes, cada 15 minutos de 9:00 a 17:45, en
     los segundos 0 y 30». **A bare cadence of four seconds or fewer is
     named, never a cadence**: «en los segundos 0, 15, 30 y 45».
     **After a time part in several pieces, or one that ends in an
     exception, the clause opens «siempre»**: `15,45 0-29,45 9 * * *` is
     «todos los días, cada minuto de 9:00 a 9:29 y a las 9:45, siempre en
     los segundos 15 y 45». Without it the seconds are heard with the last
     piece only («a las 9:45, en los segundos 15 y 45»). A time part is in
     several pieces when an «y» joins anything in it.
   * Seconds that fill a stretch say for how long: every second, «una vez
     por segundo durante todo ese minuto»; a bare cadence of five seconds
     or more, «una vez cada 6 segundos durante todo ese minuto»; a run,
     «una vez por segundo durante los primeros 10 segundos», «una vez por
     segundo del segundo 10 al 20»; a cadence of eight or more that is
     not bare, «una vez cada 7 segundos del segundo 0 al 56».
   * Seconds in runs and cadences together (3.10) are a stretch too, each
     piece with «una vez»: «…, y en cada ocasión una vez cada 2 segundos
     del segundo 0 al 20 y una vez cada 3 segundos del segundo 21 al 57».
   * Seconds of both kinds are joined as pieces are, each said in its
     own way, and the clause is led as a stretch is: «…, y en cada
     ocasión una vez por segundo durante los primeros 10 segundos y en
     el segundo 30».
   * After a time part that is only clock times such a stretch follows a
     comma: «a las 9:00, una vez por segundo durante todo ese minuto».
     After any other time part it follows «, y en cada ocasión»: «cada 15
     minutos de 9:00 a 17:45, y en cada ocasión una vez por segundo
     durante todo ese minuto». Without those words reader 3 read the
     third version's sentence as, possibly, one burst of a minute a day.

The history of the clause. «, y cada vez cada segundo durante un minuto»
(second version): "no lo escribe nadie", and three «cada» in five words
(readers 2 and schedule). A bare comma and «una vez por segundo durante
un minuto» (third version): understood after a clock time, with half a
re-read "para ver que el minuto es el de las 9:00", and re-read after a
cadence. So «un minuto» became «todo ese minuto», which points at the
minute just named, and after a cadence the clause says «en cada
ocasión», which has no «cada vez» and no second «cada» beside it.
Rejected: «30 segundos después de la hora en punto» and «dos veces: en
punto y 30 segundos después», which are right for those values and do
not extend to a list or a run.

4.3 A day of the month and a weekday. Cron fires when either matches.
**The weekdays and the time come first, as they would stand with no day
of the month; then a comma, «y además», and the day-of-month phrase.**

* `0 0 1 * 5` — «los viernes a las 0:00, y además el día 1 de cada mes»
* `0 0 1 * 1-5` — «de lunes a viernes a las 0:00, y además el día 1 de
  cada mes»
* `*/15 9-17 1-7,15 * 1-5` — «de lunes a viernes, cada 15 minutos de 9:00
  a 17:45, y además del 1 al 7 y el día 15 de cada mes»
* `0 0 1 * 5L` — «el último viernes de cada mes a las 0:00, y además el
  día 1 de cada mes»
* `*/15 9-17 1,3,…,13,17,…,31 * 1-5` — «de lunes a viernes, cada 15
  minutos de 9:00 a 17:45, y además los días impares del 1 al 13 y del 17
  al 31 de cada mes»

The time is said once, with the first side, and the addition takes it
over as any speaker hears it («los viernes a las 0:00, y además el día
1»). Whatever the time part ends in (an exception, a seconds clause) is
closed by the comma before «y además».

**Under named months, the months lead; the weekday side follows them as
it would alone, and the days of the month say «de ese mes» (one month)
or «de esos meses» (several) in place of «de cada mes».** `0 0 1 6 5` is
«en junio, los viernes a las 0:00, y además el día 1 de ese mes»; `0 0
1,15 1,7 1-5`, «en enero y julio, de lunes a viernes a las 0:00, y además
los días 1 y 15 de esos meses»; `0 0 L 6-8 5`, «de junio a agosto, los
viernes a las 0:00, y además el último día de esos meses». In a union no
side takes a month into itself: a Quartz weekday side says «del mes»
after the leading months, «en junio, el segundo lunes del mes a las 0:00,
y además el día 1 de ese mes».

**Neither side takes an exception.** The side that is a set of weekdays
never did (3.9). The days of the month took one until the second repair:
`0 9 1-14,16-31 * 5` was «los viernes a las 9:00, y además todos los días
de cada mes, excepto el día 15». An exception at the end of a sentence is
heard against everything before it, and here that is wrong: a Friday that
falls on the 15th fires. So in a union the days are said positively, as
runs, lists and cadences in their stretches (3.10): «los viernes a las
9:00, y además del 1 al 14 y del 16 al 31 de cada mes»; `0 0 1-30 * 1`,
«los lunes a las 0:00, y además del 1 al 30 de cada mes». The usage notes
say the same of any exception: it belongs to what is just before it, and
where it could be read against two things the sentence is recast.

**Days of the month that are every day of the months named are no
union.** `0 0 1-30 6 1` fires on every day of June, and is «en junio,
todos los días a las 0:00»: the Mondays add nothing. The core states
this (`dateReach.days.everyDay`), with the years taken into account:
`0 0 0 1-28 2 * 2031` is «en febrero de 2031, todos los días a las 0:00».

**A day of the month that no named month has leaves the weekdays
alone.** `0 0 31 6 1` is «los lunes de junio a las 0:00», exactly as with
no day of the month.

**Where the day exists in some of the named months only, the weekdays
keep the months as written and the addition names its own.** A month
phrase with no run in it joins the date, and a run leads it between
commas: `0 0 31 4,5 1` is «en abril y mayo, los lunes a las 0:00, y
además el 31 de mayo»; `0 0 30 2-12 1`, «de febrero a diciembre, los
lunes a las 0:00, y además, de marzo a diciembre, el día 30 de cada mes».
(«De esos meses» would promise a 31st of April.)

Why this order. Three fluent readers judged the earlier forms, «el día 1
de cada mes, y también de lunes a viernes, a las 0:00» and, under months,
«en enero y julio, los días 1 y 15, y también de lunes a viernes durante
esos meses, a las 0:00». Each found a second reading: that the 1st counts
only if it falls on a working day; that the time covers the weekday side
only; that the weekdays run all year. Reader 3: "pone delante lo raro (el
día 1) y detrás lo habitual". And every rewrite, from every reader, had
the same shape: the frequent rule with its time first, the dates after it
as the addition («de lunes a viernes a las 0:00, y además el día 1 de
cada mes»). «Y además» is their word; «y también» was called the seam of
a template. Their rewrites usually ended «aunque caiga en fin de semana»,
which is true only when the weekdays are Monday to Friday; the general
«caiga en el día que caiga» was considered and left out, because after a
complete clause «y además» already adds. A fluent reviewer may want it.

The cost to continuity is declared (2.1, 2.7): restricting the weekday of
a dated schedule moves the date phrase from before the time to after it,
unchanged in its words: «el día 1 de cada mes a las 0:00» becomes «los
viernes a las 0:00, y además el día 1 de cada mes». Restricting the day
of the month of a weekday schedule only appends.

**When both day fields must match, the date leads and the weekdays are
a condition on it.** The core says it (`day.both`): a field written from a
star (`*/2`) beside a restricted one. The days of the month are said as
they are said alone, with their months, and after them, between commas,
«cuando cae en» (one date) or «cuando caen en» (several) and the
weekdays, then the time. The days take no exception and the weekdays are
no exception either; the words «y además» never appear, so the sentence
cannot be heard as a union. `0 0 */2 * 1` is «los días impares de cada
mes, cuando caen en lunes, a las 0:00»; `0 9 */2 * 1,3,5`, «los días
impares de cada mes, cuando caen en lunes, miércoles o viernes, a las
9:00»; `0 0 15 * */2`, «el día 15 de cada mes, cuando cae en martes,
jueves, sábado o domingo, a las 0:00»; `0 0 */2 6 1`, «los días impares
de junio, cuando caen en lunes, a las 0:00»; `*/15 9-17 */2 * 1-5`, «los
días impares de cada mes, cuando caen de lunes a viernes, cada 15 minutos
de 9:00 a 17:45». The weekdays are a list joined by «o»; a run is «de
lunes a viernes» and takes no «en», other days follow it in the same
list («de lunes a miércoles, viernes o sábado»); Saturday and Sunday are
«en fin de semana». A weekday of the month is not a set to fall on: «los
días impares de cada mes, cuando coinciden con el último viernes del
mes». A date of a Quartz form leads as it would alone: `0 0 L * */2`,
«el último día de cada mes, cuando cae en martes, jueves, sábado o
domingo». Months and years sit where they always do. Written without the
star (`1-31/2`) the same days are a union, and say «y además» as before.
The seed said «los viernes que caen en día 13», the weekday first; the
date leads instead because its phrase is then the one it has alone, for
every kind of set (a cadence, a run, a Quartz form), and the weekdays
stay one short list. This is a declared fusion (2.7): editing either day
field changes the day part only, as in a union.

4.4 A day of the month, a month, and a year. A **date phrase** is a
day-of-month phrase («el día 1 de cada mes») or a Quartz form («el último
día de cada mes», «el segundo lunes de cada mes»). Every date phrase says
«de cada mes» when no month is named **(schedule reader:** the second
version said «del mes» for some forms and «de cada mes» for others, both
in one sentence in a union**)**.

* With every month: «el día 1 de cada mes»; «los días 1 y 15 de cada mes»;
  «del 1 al 7 de cada mes»; «los días impares de cada mes».
* **A month phrase with no range in it (one month, or a list of months)
  joins the date phrase**, «de» and the months in place of «de cada mes»
, as a date is written. One month: «el 1 de junio» (one day
  takes no «día»); «los días 1 y 15 de junio»; «del 1 al 7 de junio»;
  «del 1 al 7 y el 15 de junio»; «los días impares de junio»; «el último
  día de junio»; «los días pares del 8 al 30 de junio». A single day
  inside pieces keeps «día», so that adding the month changes only the
  month's words: «del 1 al 7 y el día 15 de junio». A list: «el día 1 de
  enero y julio»; «el día 1 de marzo, junio, septiembre y diciembre»; «los
  días 1 y 15 de enero y julio»; «el último viernes de enero y julio»;
  every second month, «el día 1 de enero, marzo, mayo, julio, septiembre
  y noviembre».
* **Days the named months do not have are not said.** A plain day that a
  month lacks does not fire, so under named months a list, range or
  cadence of days holds only the days that at least one of those months
  has (the core states them, `dateReach.days`), described afresh: `0 0
  */5 6 *` is «los días 1, 6, 11, 16, 21 y 26 de junio»; `0 0 25-31 6 *`,
  «del 25 al 30 de junio»; `0 0 30,31 4,6 *`, «el día 30 de abril y
  junio»; `0 0 1,31 4,6,9,11 *`, «el día 1 de abril, junio, septiembre y
  noviembre». With every month nothing is cut: «el día 31 de cada mes» is
  taken by every reader as skipping the months without one, and so is a
  list that ends in 31.
* **Where a named month has none of the days, only the months that fire
  are said** (the core states them, `dateReach.everyYear` and
  `leapYears`), by the same rules as any months: `0 0 30 2-3 *` is «el
  30 de marzo a las 0:00»; `0 0 31 1-6 *`, «el día 31 de enero, marzo y
  mayo a las 0:00»; `0 0 30 2-6 *`, «de marzo a junio, el día 30 de cada
  mes a las 0:00». This is a declared fusion (2.7): the day of the month
  changes which months are named. The first repair kept the months as
  written and added which of them fire («en febrero y marzo, en los meses
  que tienen día 30, …»); that says the month and takes it back.
* **A day that no month in reach has: the schedule never fires, and the
  sentence says so and why.** «Nunca, porque» and the months, «no tiene»
  or «no tienen», «día» and the days, joined with «ni» as a list after a
  negation is: `0 0 31 6 *` is «nunca, porque junio no tiene día 31»;
  `0 0 30,31 2 *`, «nunca, porque febrero no tiene día 30 ni 31»; `0 0
  31 4,6 *`, «nunca, porque abril y junio no tienen día 31». A single
  year follows the month's name, and any other year phrase comes before
  the months: `0 0 0 29 2 * 2031` is «nunca, porque febrero de 2031 no
  tiene día 29»; with 2031 and 2033, «nunca, porque en 2031 y 2033
  febrero no tiene día 29». No time is said: there is none at which it
  runs (2.7). As a sentence the negative goes before the verb: «No se
  ejecuta nunca, porque junio no tiene día 31.» Rejected: the date as
  written («el día 31 de junio a las 0:00»), which names a date that does
  not exist and lets the reader think the schedule runs; «nunca» alone,
  which looks like a failure of the description.
* **A 29th of February with no other month fires in leap years only, and
  leads with «en los años bisiestos»**, after any years and before the
  date: `0 0 29 2 *` is «en los años bisiestos, el 29 de febrero a las
  0:00»; with years, «de 2030 a 2040, en los años bisiestos, el 29 de
  febrero a las 0:00»; in a union, inside the addition, «en febrero, los
  lunes a las 0:00, y además, en los años bisiestos, el día 29 de ese
  mes». Every reader knows it of the date itself, and the sentence says
  it because the schedule's whole rhythm is that one fact. Beside other
  months nothing is added: «el día 29 de febrero y marzo» is read as «el
  día 31 de cada mes» is. Where the years reached are leap years only,
  or never leap, the core says so and there is nothing to add: `0 0 0 29
  2 * 2032` is «el 29 de febrero de 2032 a las 0:00».
* **A month phrase with a range in it leads**, and the date phrase stays
  whole: «de junio a agosto, el día 1 de cada mes a las 0:00»; «de junio a
  agosto y en diciembre, el último día de cada mes a las 0:00». **(Reader 1**
  stumbled on «el día 1 de cada mes de junio a agosto», "se lee «cada mes
  de junio»".**)** A range cannot join the date: «el día 1 de junio a
  agosto» reads as a range of dates.
* In a union every month phrase leads (4.3).
* **A single year follows a month's name with «de»**, wherever the month
  stands, when the month phrase is one piece (one month, one list or one
  run). After months in several pieces the year would be heard with the
  last piece only, so it leads: `0 0 9 * 1-5,7-12 * 2030` is «en 2030, de
  enero a mayo y de julio a diciembre, todos los días a las 9:00». One
  piece: «el 1 de enero de 2030 a las 0:00»; «los días 1 y 15 de junio de
  2030»; «los lunes de junio de 2030»; «en junio de 2030, todos los días a
  las 9:00»; «de junio a agosto de 2030, el día 1 de cada mes a las 0:00».
* **Any other year phrase leads the sentence**, before the months: «en
  2030, todos los días a las 9:00»; «en 2030, el día 1 de cada mes a las
  9:00»; «en 2030 y 2035, el 1 de enero a las 0:00»; «de 2030 a 2035, el 1
  de enero a las 0:00»; «de 2030 a 2032 y en 2035, …»; «en 2030 y 2031, en
  junio, todos los días a las 9:00». **(Reader 1** on «en junio de 2030 y de
  2031»: "un lector rápido puede dudar de si es todo 2031".**)** A single
  year with no month named leads too.

4.5 A weekday and a month. A weekday phrase that is «los» and one day or
a list of days (also «los fines de semana») takes a month phrase with no
range in it, with «de»: `0 9 * 6 1` is «los lunes de junio a las 9:00»;
«los lunes, miércoles y viernes de junio»; «los fines de semana de
junio»; «los lunes de enero y julio»; with a year, «los lunes de junio de
2030». **(Reader 1:** «los lunes en junio» "es calco del inglés"; «los
lunes de junio» "es lo único idiomático".**)** A weekday phrase with a
range in it does not take the month, which leads: «en junio, de lunes a
viernes a las 9:00»; «en junio, de martes a viernes y los domingos a las
9:00» («de lunes a viernes de junio» would be heard as running to a
date). A month phrase with a range in it always leads: «de junio a
agosto, los lunes a las 9:00». So does any month when there is no day
part or it is «todos los días»: «en junio, todos los días a las 9:00»;
«en junio, cada 5 minutos».

## 5. Idioms

5.1 Days of the month 1, 3, 5 … 31: «los días impares». Days 2, 4 … 30:
«los días pares». Each can take exceptions (3.9). **(Reader 1** read «los
días impares de cada mes, excepto el 15» exactly and without
complaint.**)**

5.2 Saturday and Sunday: «los fines de semana». Monday to Friday has no
idiom: it is the range «de lunes a viernes», which is what a speaker
says, and which means those five days whatever the calendar of public
holidays says. No word for a kind of day is used («laborable», «hábil»,
«entre semana», «festivo»): each means a set of days the pattern does not
know (5.7).

5.3 Midnight and noon are written as times, «a las 0:00» and «a las
12:00», in both clock styles (with `hour12: true`, «a las 12:00 a. m.»
and «a las 12:00 p. m.»; 1.2 has the doubt about those). **(Reader 1:**
«a medianoche el día 1» "es peligrosa: muchos la leen como la noche del 1
al 2".**)** Reader 2 missed «mediodía» and «medianoche», and also noted
that «a las 0:00» of a named day does not say which midnight; 0:00 is the
first minute of the day it is written with, and both readers took it so.

5.4 Minute 0 with every hour is «cada hora en punto»: «los lunes, cada
hora en punto»; «cada hora en punto, excepto a las 3:00». Until the
second repair it was «cada hora», the one cadence left with nothing to
say when in the hour it lands (the usage notes give «a las horas en
punto» as what a speaker says, and «en punto» is their word for minute
0). «Cada hora de 9:00 a 17:00» needs no «en punto»: its clock times say
it.

5.5 Every second hour as points names both ends (3.7); filled by minutes
it is «durante una hora cada 2 horas» with its bracket (4.1). Every
second month is listed. Every second day, year, and off-beat minute or
second is «pares» or «impares» (5.1, 5.8, 3.7). Every other interval is
a digit.

5.6 None by default. «A. m.» and «p. m.» are used only with `hour12:
true`.

5.7 Quartz forms. Each is tied to the months as a date is: «de cada mes»,
or «de» and the months where they join it (4.4).

* `L` — «el último día de cada mes»; «el último día de junio»
* `L-1` — «el penúltimo día de cada mes»; `L-2` — «el antepenúltimo día
  de cada mes»
* `L-3`, and every larger number — «3 días antes del último día de cada
  mes», closed by a comma (2.1): `0 0 L-3 * *` is «3 días antes del último
  día de cada mes, a las 0:00»; with June, «3 días antes del último día de
  junio, a las 0:00». No wording here has satisfied every reader. Readers
  1 and 2 had to subtract, and one saw "el antepenúltimo" in it; the third
  version's «cuando faltan 3 días para el último día de cada mes a las
  0:00» was worse for reader 3: «cuando faltan» "suena a condición, no a
  fecha", «a las 0:00» "parece colgar de «el último día»", and one reading
  counted the last day. Reader 3's own form is this one with the comma,
  and the comma is what was missing before. Rejected: «tres días antes de
  fin de mes», which can be counted from the day after the last.
* `1#2` — «el segundo lunes de cada mes»; «el segundo lunes de junio»
* `5L` — «el último viernes de cada mes»

The ordinals are «primer, segundo, tercer, cuarto, quinto»; every weekday
is masculine.

**The Monday-to-Friday forms name their days.** `W` means the day from
Monday to Friday nearest a date, public holidays included. Until the
second repair these said «día laborable» (and «día hábil» in the `mx`
style): «el día laborable más cercano al 15 de cada mes», «el último día
laborable de cada mes». The usage notes show both words mean another set
of days to their readers. «Laborable» is ambiguous about Saturday (a
timetable's «laborables» stops on Friday; the labour calendar's includes
Saturday), and «día hábil» is defined by each country's law, which has
moved Saturday in and out. Neither includes a public holiday, and the
pattern fires on one. The notes' advice is to write the days, and their
device for a date that moves is «caer»: «si cae en festivo, pasa al
siguiente día hábil». So the form says the date, and where it goes when
it falls on Saturday or Sunday:

* `15W` — «el día 15 de cada mes o, si cae en sábado o domingo, el
  viernes o el lunes más cercano». A 15th on a Saturday gives Friday the
  14th; on a Sunday, Monday the 16th. With one month it is written as a
  date: «el 15 de junio o, si cae en sábado o domingo, el viernes o el
  lunes más cercano». With a list of months, «el día 15 de marzo, junio,
  septiembre y diciembre o, si cae en sábado o domingo, …».
* `1W` — «el día 1 de cada mes o, si cae en sábado o domingo, el lunes
  siguiente». The day never leaves its month, so a 1st on a Saturday
  gives Monday the 3rd, not the Friday before. (The core reports `1W` as
  its own form.)
* `LW` — «el último día de cada mes o, si cae en sábado o domingo, el
  viernes anterior».
* **Days 28 to 31 can be a month's last day**, where a Sunday cannot give
  the Monday after: the Friday or Monday is «del mismo mes». `28W` is «el
  día 28 de cada mes o, si cae en sábado o domingo, el viernes o el lunes
  más cercano del mismo mes»; `30W` in June, «el 30 de junio o, si cae en
  sábado o domingo, el viernes o el lunes más cercano del mismo mes». Days
  2 to 27 cannot reach an edge of any month and say nothing of it.
* The phrase is closed by a comma before the time (2.1): «…, el viernes
  anterior, a las 0:00». In a union it is the addition as it stands: «los
  viernes a las 0:00, y además el día 15 de cada mes o, si cae en sábado o
  domingo, el viernes o el lunes más cercano».
* With `short: true` the weekday names are short here as everywhere: «o,
  si cae en sáb o dom, el vie anterior».

A month without day n is skipped, never answered with the end of the
shorter month. Said as a date that moves, the form needs nothing more for
that: «el día 31 de cada mes o, si cae …» is read as «el día 31 de cada
mes» is (4.4), where the first repair's «el día laborable más cercano al
31» promised a fire in April and needed «en los meses que tienen día 31»
in front of it.

Rejected: «día entre semana» («entre semana» is adverbial, and the two
earlier readers split on it); «día de lunes a viernes», which "no se
dice"; «el último día del mes que caiga de lunes a viernes», exact for
`LW` but with no counterpart for «más cercano al 15»; a gloss, «día
laborable (de lunes a viernes)», which reader 2 marked down in every
sentence and which still says the doubtful word. The earlier readers
ranked «el último día laborable del mes» first as "como se escribe de
verdad"; it is, and it means the last day the reader's office is open.

**Which months fire** (the core states them, `dateReach`):

* Under named months only the months that fire are said, as for any date
  (4.4): `0 0 31W 6-8 *` is «el día 31 de julio y agosto o, si cae en
  sábado o domingo, el viernes o el lunes más cercano del mismo mes, a
  las 0:00»; `0 0 30W 2,4 *`, «el 30 de abril o, si cae …»; `0 0 L-30 6-8
  *`, «30 días antes del último día de julio y agosto, a las 0:00»; `0 0
  L-29 2,4 *`, «29 días antes del último día de abril, a las 0:00».
* Where no month in reach has the day, the schedule never fires (4.4):
  `0 0 31W 6 *` is «nunca, porque junio no tiene día 31»; `0 0 L-30 6 *`,
  «nunca, porque junio no tiene más de 30 días».
* A February alone that has the day only in leap years leads with «en los
  años bisiestos» (4.4): `0 0 29W 2 *` is «en los años bisiestos, el 29 de
  febrero o, si cae en sábado o domingo, el viernes o el lunes más cercano
  del mismo mes, a las 0:00»; `0 0 L-28 2 *`, «en los años bisiestos, 28
  días antes del último día de febrero, a las 0:00».
* **A day counted from the last (`L-3` and up) names no date, so a reader
  cannot see which months are long enough.** Where the months said do not
  settle it (every month, or a February beside other months), the
  sentence leads with the months that fire, and the day says «del mes»:
  `0 0 L-30 * *` is «en los meses de más de 30 días, 30 días antes del
  último día del mes, a las 0:00»; `0 0 L-28 2,3 *`, «en febrero y marzo,
  en los meses de más de 28 días, 28 días antes del último día del mes, a
  las 0:00». In a union the phrase opens the addition, between commas
  (2.1): `0 0 L-30 * 5` is «los viernes a las 0:00, y además, en los
  meses de más de 30 días, 30 días antes del último día del mes».
* In a union under named months the addition names the months it fires
  in (4.3): `0 0 31W 6,7 5` is «en junio y julio, los viernes a las 0:00,
  y además el 31 de julio o, si cae en sábado o domingo, el viernes o el
  lunes más cercano del mismo mes».

5.8 Years. Years 1970, 1972 … : «en los años pares». Years 1971, 1973 … :
«en los años impares». **(Reader 1:** "«años pares» sí se dice".**)** Every
second year from a later start keeps the idiom and names the start, or
both ends if it stops early: «en los años pares desde 2030»; «en los años
impares desde 2031»; «en los años pares de 2030 a 2040»; from the first
year there is to a last, «en los años pares hasta 2030».

**The years have a first year (1970) and an end no one will see (9999),
and neither is named.** The core marks a run or a series that goes on to
the end as open. A run with no end is «desde» and its first year: `2030/1`
is «desde 2030, todos los días a las 9:00» (it was «de 2030 a 9999»). A
run from the first year is «hasta» and its last: `1970-2030` is «hasta
2030, todos los días a las 9:00». Such a run names one thing, so every
year from 1971 is «desde 1971», not «todos los años, excepto en 1970».
Pieces: «en 2030 y 2031, y desde 2033»; «de 2030 a 2040 y desde 2050».

Any other cadence of years is listed up to seven years, «en 2028, 2032,
2036 y 2040». From eight it is a series: its first three years, «y así
sucesivamente», and its last year if it has one: «en 2028, 2032, 2036 y
así sucesivamente»; «en 2028, 2032, 2036 y así sucesivamente hasta 2060».

Years that are a cadence with a hole in the middle take an exception, by
the count of 3.9 (more than seven years, fewer things named): «excepto
en» and the years, closed by the comma every leading phrase has. `2000-
2028/2,2032-2098/2` is «en los años pares de 2000 a 2098, excepto en
2030, todos los días a las 9:00»; `2028-2036/4,2044-2060/4`, «en 2028,
2032, 2036 y así sucesivamente hasta 2060, excepto en 2040, …». Without
it the first of these was a list of forty-nine years. The excepted year
is never one of the three the series shows (3.9): `2028,2036-2060/4` is
«en 2028 y en 2036, 2040, 2044 y así sucesivamente hasta 2060». Every
year but a few is «todos los años, excepto en 2030». Years in runs with
a hole are pieces, never an exception: «de 2030 a 2034 y de 2036 a
2040».

**Runs and series together** (3.10): `2030/2,2031` is «en los años pares
desde 2030 y en 2031, todos los días a las 9:00»; `2030-2040,2045/5`, «de
2030 a 2040 y en 2045, 2050, 2055 y así sucesivamente, …»; `2030/4,2031/4`,
«en 2030, 2034, 2038 y así sucesivamente, y en 2031, 2035, 2039 y así
sucesivamente, …». Before this rule each of these was a list of hundreds
or thousands of years. A list of years is never longer than the seven a
short cadence has, plus the loose years the pattern itself names.

* `0 0 9 * * * 2028/4` — «en 2028, 2032, 2036 y así sucesivamente, todos
  los días a las 9:00»
* `0 */5 * * * * 2028/4` — «en 2028, 2032, 2036 y así sucesivamente, cada
  5 minutos»

The history: «en un año de cada 4 desde 2028» "obliga a releer" (reader
1); «cada 4 años desde 2028» after «todos los días» "se contradice"
(reader 1); «en 2028, 2032, 2036 y así cada 4 años», the second version,
was understood exactly but «y así» alone "es de conversación, no de
pantalla" (reader 2). «Y así sucesivamente» is the written form of the
same thing. Reader 2's «cada 4 años a partir de 2028 (2028, 2032, 2036…)»
was not taken: it says the interval and then the list, and needs a
bracket.

## 6. Words

6.1 Syllables.

6.2 Numbers are written as digits.

6.3 Months and weekdays are in the lexicon, with their short forms.
Weekdays ending in «s» do not change in the plural («los lunes»); «los
sábados» and «los domingos» do.

6.4 segundo(s), minuto(s), hora(s), día(s), mes(es), año(s).

6.5 a, a la, a las, en, el, los, las, de, del, al, y, o, ni, y además, y
así sucesivamente, y en cada ocasión, siempre, cada, una vez por, una vez
cada, durante, desde, hasta, hasta las, excepto, todos los días, todos los
años, primeros, últimos, último, penúltimo, antepenúltimo, pares, impares,
que empiezan, todo ese minuto, una hora, en punto, antes del, o si cae
en, anterior, siguiente, más cercano, del mismo mes, de ese mes, de esos
meses, en los años bisiestos, en los meses de más de, más de, nunca,
porque, no tiene, no tienen, de cada hora, de cada minuto, de cada mes,
del mes. The sentence wrapper has «Se ejecuta» and, for a schedule that
never fires, «No se ejecuta».

## 7. Agreement

7.1 Singular for 1, plural otherwise: «1 día», «3 días». After «cada» with
no number the unit is singular: «cada minuto». No stretch of one is ever
said: a first or last stretch of one minute is that minute («en el minuto
0 de cada hora»), and one day before the last is «el penúltimo día». So
«días antes» is always plural.

7.2 Gender and number: «el minuto», «los minutos»; «la hora», «las horas»;
«el día», «los días»; «el segundo». «Primeros» and «últimos» agree with
«minutos» and «segundos».

7.3 «De» and «el» contract to «del», «a» and «el» to «al»: «del 1 al 7»,
«del minuto 10 al 40», «del mes». «A la» before hour 1; «hasta las»
before any hour, and «hasta la» before hour 1.

7.4 A list: commas, then «y» before the last item, with no comma before
it. Two items: «A y B».

7.5 None. Dates take no ordinal mark («el 1 de junio»).

7.6 Left to right, Western digits, words separated by single spaces.

## 8. Traps

8.1 `union-connective` — «, y además»: «los viernes a las 0:00, y además
el día 1 de cada mes».

8.2 `shared-qualifier-scope` — In a union the time is said with the
first side and the addition takes it over. A month leads, and the
addition points back to it: «en junio, los viernes a las 0:00, y además
el día 1 de ese mes». Neither side of a union takes an exception (4.3).
Named seconds after a time part in several pieces say «siempre» (4.2). A
single year follows a month's name only when the months are one piece
(4.4). A piece with an «y» of its own is closed by a comma before the
«y» that joins the last piece (2.1).

8.3 `confinement-vs-juxtaposition` — A confinement is a window («de … a
…»), «durante una hora cada N horas» with its bracket, «durante las
horas que empiezan a las …», or «durante todo ese minuto». The commas
are those of 2.1, the one before a window after «de cada hora», and the
one before the seconds clause.

8.4 `redundancy` — «De cada mes» is not said beside a single named month.
A year the field cannot go beyond is not named («desde 2030», «hasta
2030»). A month in which the day never fires is not named.
«Cada minuto» is not said after «cada segundo». A field that takes every
value is not said.

8.5 `range-boundary` — Every range of times is closed, to the precision
of the phrase it bounds. Under «cada minuto», a cadence of minutes or a
minute phrase, it names the first and the last minute in which the
schedule fires; under a seconds lead («cada segundo de 9:00:00 a
17:59:59») it names the first and last second. A seconds clause after a
window to the minute says when inside each minute («…, de 3:30 a 10:42»
after «cada segundo durante los minutos 30 y 42 de cada hora» ends at
10:42:59). There is no form in which a time that is named does not fire,
and none in which a window names a moment that an exception then removes
(3.9).

8.6 `recurrence-marking` — «Los» and the plural for single days and lists
(«los lunes y miércoles»); «de … a …» with no article for a run; «todos
los días, excepto» for six days that wrap the week; «los fines de
semana».

8.7 `numeral-register` — Digits for every count and position.

8.8 `sentence-wrapper-punctuation` — A label starts with a capital
letter: «Todos los días a las 9:00», «Cada minuto». A sentence is «Se
ejecuta » before the description and a full stop after: «Se ejecuta todos
los días a las 9:00.» If the description already ends in a full stop (as
«p. m.» does), no second one is added. The description for `@reboot` is
«al iniciar el sistema». The lenient fallback is «un patrón cron no
reconocido», which is never wrapped.

8.9 `cardinality-rendering` — Decided by the counts of the opening
section. `0 0 1 */3 *` is «el día 1 de enero, abril, julio y octubre a
las 0:00»; `0 0 1 */2 *` is «el día 1 de enero, marzo, mayo, julio,
septiembre y noviembre a las 0:00»; `0 */6 * * *` and `0 0,6,12,18 * * *`
are both «todos los días a las 0:00, 6:00, 12:00 y 18:00»; `0 */2 * * *`
is «todos los días, cada 2 horas de 0:00 a 22:00».

## 9. Where this rule book departs from the usage notes

`usage.md` was written in Spanish, after the first three versions of this
rule book, by a writer told nothing of schedules or of this project. The
second repair (2026-11-13) read the rule book against it section by
section. What was taken from the notes: the Monday-to-Friday forms name
their days and use «caer» (5.7); «en punto» for minute 0 of every hour
(5.4); «ni» in a list after a negation (4.4); no exception where it could
be read against two things (4.3); the comma before an «y» that follows a
piece with its own «y» (2.1). What agrees already: days before months
before hours, the frequency with the time; «los lunes» for a habit; «de …
a …» without the article for what recurs and «del 1 al 15» for dates; a
run from three values and a list for two; «a la 1:00»; the union said
with «y» and «además»; «en los minutos 5, 20 y 45 de cada hora» and never
«cada 15 minutos a partir del minuto 5»; days of the month by parity and
never «cada 2 días»; no «cada ocho días», «cada quince días» or «cada
tercer día», which count both ends (cadences of seven or fifteen days are
listed as dates).

The departures, each with its reason:

1. **The end of a range of hours.** The notes call «cada hora de 9:00 a
   14:00» the doubtful case (is there one at 14:00?) and cure it by naming
   the last occurrence, «(última a las 14:00)». The rule book keeps «cada
   hora de 9:00 a 17:00» with no gloss. In every form here the last time
   named is a time that fires (8.5), the windows under minutes show it by
   their ends («de 9:00 a 17:45», «de 9:00 a 17:59»), and three blind
   readers took the hourly window exactly. The gloss would repeat the end
   already named in the most common sentences. This is a judgment of the
   notes' writer against the readings of three others; a fluent reviewer
   may still want «(la última, a las 17:00)».
2. **The night of two days.** The notes say a careful text writes «la
   noche del viernes al sábado» and not «sábados de 0:00 a 2:00». A
   pattern's hours belong to the day it names: `0 22-2 * * 1` fires in
   the first hours of Monday and its last two, and «los lunes, cada hora
   de 0:00 a 2:00 y a las 22:00 y 23:00» is that. «La noche del lunes al
   martes» would be another schedule.
3. **The time in a union.** The notes put the time once at the end, «los
   viernes y el día 13 de cada mes, a las 9:00», and add that it must be
   repeated if the comma is not enough. The rule book says it after the
   first side, «los viernes a las 9:00, y además el día 13 de cada mes»:
   three readers could not tell what a final time covered once the time
   part was longer than a clock time, and each rewrote the sentence this
   way (4.3).
4. **The comma between the day and a clock time.** The notes' examples
   mostly have it («los días 1 y 15 de cada mes, a las 9:00») and say both
   are right. The rule book writes a space before a clock time and a comma
   before anything else (2.1); two readers asked for one rule, and the
   comma is kept for where a phrase has to be closed.
5. **«Cada 7 minutos».** The notes prefer listing to a cadence that the
   hour breaks. The rule book lists up to seven values and from eight
   names both ends, «cada 7 minutos del minuto 0 al 56 de cada hora»: the
   ends show where the count stops and starts again, and a list of nine
   or more is harder to hold than the break is to see.
6. **Twelve o'clock on the 12-hour clock.** The notes call «12 a. m.» and
   «12 p. m.» ambiguous and advise «12 de la noche», «12 del mediodía» or
   the 24-hour clock. The `mx` style keeps «12:00 a. m.» and «12:00
   p. m.» (1.2, 5.3): beside a date, «a las 12 de la noche del día 1»
   moves the doubt from the hour to the day, which is worse; the 24-hour
   clock is the default for whoever wants no doubt.
7. **The symbol «h» and the first of the month.** The notes recommend
   «8:00 h» and record «primero de mayo» for America. The rule book writes
   times bare («a las 9:00»), which the notes allow where the context is
   enough, and «el 1 de mayo» in both styles, which is read «primero»
   where that is said.
8. **«Los fines de semana».** The notes divide the week into three or
   four kinds of day and put Saturday on its own. The rule book keeps the
   idiom for Saturday and Sunday together (5.2): the notes use it in that
   sense themselves («el primer fin de semana del mes»), and no region
   reads another pair of days into it. It never stands for Friday night.
9. **Six days as an exception.** The notes prefer a positive run to «todos
   los días excepto los lunes» where one comes out clean. The rule book
   agrees for the two runs that do not cross the week's end («de martes a
   domingo», «de lunes a sábado») and keeps the exception for the others
   (3.9): «de jueves a martes» leaves the reader to find the missing day.
10. **Unverified claims.** The notes mark their own judgments [J] and what
    they could not check [N]. Nothing here rests on an [N] claim alone.
    The definitions of «día hábil» are [F] (the laws quoted); the reading
    of «laborable» is [J], and it only had to show a doubt to be acted
    on.
